NECO vs WAEC: Are They Really Different and Which Should You Write?

Both are accepted for university admission, but there are differences in style, timing and perception worth knowing.

The short answer

NECO and WAEC are two separate examination bodies that both issue Senior School Certificate results. Both are recognised for admission into Nigerian tertiary institutions, and both test your knowledge of the senior secondary school syllabus. For most students the real question is not which is better, but which one is available to you, which your school registers you for, and what your target institution requires.

This guide explains how they differ in practice so you can decide calmly. Details such as dates, fees and registration windows change, so check the official WAEC and NECO portals for the current information.

Who runs each exam

WAEC, the West African Examinations Council, is a regional body that serves several West African countries. Its main senior secondary exam is the West African Senior School Certificate Examination, called WASSCE. NECO, the National Examinations Council, is a Nigerian body that runs the Senior School Certificate Examination, called SSCE, in Nigeria.

Because they have different organisers, their question papers, marking teams and administration are independent of each other.

What is the same

  • Both examine the senior secondary curriculum in subjects such as English, Mathematics, sciences, arts and social sciences.
  • Both issue results graded from A1 down to F9, where credit passes are generally the grades from A1 to C6.
  • Both are accepted by Nigerian universities, polytechnics and colleges of education as O-level qualifications, subject to each school's own rules.
  • Both usually combine objective questions with theory or essay questions in many subjects.
📌
Credit pass

A credit generally means a grade between A1 and C6. Most admission requirements ask for credits in English, Mathematics and subjects relevant to your course. Confirm the exact requirement with your target school.

Where they differ in practice

The differences are mostly about administration and experience, not about quality.

Timing and availability

The two exams are held at different times of the year, and each has its own routes for school candidates and private candidates. Check both official portals for the current schedule. A candidate whose results will be needed by a certain admission deadline should plan around when results are released.

Question style and emphasis

Both cover the same broad curriculum, but individual papers can differ in style, structure and the way questions are phrased. The best way to know what each looks like is to practise with real past questions from the one you plan to write, not with rumours about which is easier.

Perception

Some people believe one is more respected than the other. In formal terms, admission offices accept both. Where a school states a preference, it is in its published requirements. Rely on those, not on hearsay.

How to decide which to write

1Check what your school registers you for
2Read your target course and school requirements
3Compare the exam dates with your admission plan
4Decide whether a second sitting is needed
5Prepare using the past questions of that exam

For most SS3 students, the school decides: the school registers its candidates for one of the exams. If you are a private candidate or retaking, you have more choice. In that case, look at these factors.

  • Admission requirements: does the course or school say anything about which results it accepts or how many sittings?
  • Your timeline: when do you need the result in hand?
  • Your readiness: can you prepare well in time for the next available sitting?
  • Cost and logistics: check current fees and centre locations on the official portals.

A worked example of the decision

QuestionTunde finished SS3 with a D7 in Mathematics in his school exam. He wants Computer Science and needs a credit in Mathematics. Both exams are available to private candidates. What should he do?
  1. Check the target school requirement for the course: credits in English and Mathematics, and the number of sittings allowed
  2. Find the next available sitting for each exam and the result release time
  3. Choose the one that fits the admission timeline and that he can prepare for in time
  4. Spend the time before it on Mathematics only: weak topics first, then full past papers
  5. Register using the official portal only, and keep proof of registration
AnswerHe should pick by requirement and timeline, then focus his preparation on raising Mathematics to a credit

The decision rests on facts he can check, not on opinions about which exam is easier.

Common myths

Myth

  • One of them is useless
  • Results can be bought safely
  • The easier exam guarantees a credit
  • You should switch exams at the last minute

Reality

  • Both are recognised qualifications
  • Malpractice risks cancellation and your future
  • Preparation decides your grade
  • Stick with one plan and prepare well

Be careful about anyone offering "special centres" or guaranteed passes. These schemes can lead to cancelled results and may carry legal consequences. Real preparation is cheaper and safer.

What to do today

The best choice is the one you can prepare for and that your target school accepts.

  • Find the O-level requirements for your target course and school
  • Ask your school which exam it registers you for
  • Open the WAEC and NECO portals and note the current schedules
  • Download past questions for the exam you will write
  • Make a list of subjects where you need a credit and start with the weakest

Frequently asked questions

Is NECO accepted for university admission in Nigeria?

Yes, NECO SSCE results are widely accepted as O-level qualifications for admission in Nigerian institutions. Still, check the specific requirement of your target school and course.

Can I write both NECO and WAEC?

Many candidates do, for example school candidates in one and private candidates in the other. Check the current registration rules and make sure you can prepare properly for both.

Which is harder, NECO or WAEC?

People argue about this, but there is no reliable single answer. Both follow similar syllabuses. Your preparation matters more than the label.

Does it matter if I use results from different sittings?

Some schools restrict the number of sittings or accept combined results only in certain cases. Always read your target school's admission rules.

Who runs WAEC and NECO?

WAEC is the West African Examinations Council, a regional body that runs the WASSCE. NECO is the National Examinations Council, a Nigerian body that runs the SSCE.
