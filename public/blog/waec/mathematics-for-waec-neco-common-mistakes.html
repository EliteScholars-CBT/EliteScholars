Mathematics in WAEC and NECO: Ten Mistakes That Cost Marks

Most lost marks are not from lack of knowledge but from preventable errors. Learn to avoid them.

Michael Joseph
·📅 10 November 2026·⏱ 6 min read

Ask a teacher why students lose marks in Mathematics and the answer is rarely "they did not know the topic". It is more often "they knew it, but made a small error". Signs, brackets, units and instructions quietly eat marks. The good news is that these errors are predictable, so you can train yourself to avoid them. Here are ten, with corrections.

Mistakes with algebra and signs

1. Losing a minus sign. When you multiply a bracket by a negative number, the sign applies to every term.

QuestionExpand and simplify: 2x - 3(x - 4)
  1. Multiply -3 into both terms: -3x and +12
  2. Write the full line: 2x - 3x + 12
  3. Collect like terms: (2x - 3x) = -x
Answer-x + 12, which can also be written 12 - x. The usual wrong answer is 2x - 3x - 12, which forgets that -3 times -4 is +12.

2. Squaring a bracket term by term. (a + b)² is not a² + b². It means (a + b)(a + b), which gives a² + 2ab + b². Test with a = 1, b = 2: the left side is 9, while 1 + 4 = 5 is wrong.

3. Cancelling the wrong things. You can cancel factors, not terms. In (6x + 3)/3, you cannot cancel the 3s directly. Divide each term: 2x + 1.

4. Not moving terms correctly. When a term crosses the equals sign, its sign changes. Solve 5x - 7 = 3x + 9: collect x terms to get 2x = 16, so x = 8. Check: 5(8) - 7 = 33 and 3(8) + 9 = 33.

Mistakes with numbers and percentages

5. Mishandling fractions. Do not add the top numbers and the bottom numbers. 1/2 + 1/3 is 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6, not 2/5.

6. Treating percentage changes as reversible. A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not bring you back to the start. Begin with 100. Increase by 20% to get 120. Decrease 120 by 20%, which is 24, to get 96. The result is 4% lower than the start.

⚠️
Percent of what?

Always ask what the percentage is of. A 20% fall is 20% of the new starting value, not of the original, so rework the base each time.

7. Rounding too early or wrongly. Keep full values in your calculator until the last step, then round. Also know the difference between decimal places and significant figures. For example, 0.004560 to 2 significant figures is 0.0046, while 3.14159 to 3 significant figures is 3.14. Standard form needs one non-zero digit before the point: 45 000 = 4.5 × 10⁴.

Mistakes with units and instructions

8. Mixing units. Convert before you calculate. If a rectangle is 2.5 km by 400 m, change both to metres first: 2500 m by 400 m gives an area of 1 000 000 m². Or change both to kilometres: 2.5 km by 0.4 km gives 1 km². Both agree.

9. Ignoring the instruction. Does the question want the answer in terms of a variable, to two decimal places, in standard form or as a fraction? Circle the instruction as you read. A correct value in the wrong form can lose the final mark.

Common mistake

  • Writing answers only with no steps
  • Skipping the units in the last line
  • Leaving the calculator in the wrong mode
  • Copying numbers wrongly from the question

Better way

  • Show each step on its own line
  • End with the answer and its unit
  • Check degree mode before trigonometry
  • Underline the given values and tick them as you use them

Mistake with method and time

10. Not showing working. In theory papers, method marks matter. A wrong final answer with good steps can still earn marks, while a bare wrong answer earns nothing. Write each step on a new line and keep it tidy so the examiner can follow you. Check the official marking guidance to see how marks are allocated for your paper.

A five-step checking routine

You do not need extra study hours to fix these errors. You need a habit that takes about a minute per question.

  1. RereadLook at the question again to see what is asked and in what form
  2. Reasonable?Estimate with rounded numbers and see if your answer is sensible
  3. SubstitutePut your answer back into the original equation
  4. UnitsCheck units and the number of decimal places or significant figures
  5. Sign checkScan each line for lost or flipped signs

Practise it on past questions until it is automatic. Then, in the real exam, you will check without feeling rushed.

Keep a personal mistake list

After every practice session, write down each error and its category: signs, brackets, units, instruction, arithmetic. After two weeks you will see your own top three mistakes. Revise only those during the last week and you will fix more marks than by learning a new topic. For specific syllabus content, check the official WAEC or NECO syllabus.

What to do today

  • Do one past paper section and mark it with the ten mistakes above
  • Write your top three personal mistakes on a card
  • Practise the five-step checking routine on five questions
  • Check that your calculator is in degree mode
  • Redo any question where you lost a sign

Frequently asked questions

Do I get marks for working in WAEC and NECO maths theory?

Method marks are generally given for correct steps, so write your working clearly. Check the marking guidance from the official source for how your paper is scored.

Why do I make silly mistakes even when I know the topic?

Usually because of rushing, untidy working or not rereading the question. A fixed checking routine at the end of each question reduces them.

Should my calculator be in degree mode?

For most WAEC trigonometry, yes. Always check the mode before you start. Also confirm from the official source which calculators are allowed.

How can I check my answer quickly?

Substitute back into the original equation, estimate with rounded numbers, or test with a simple value. Each takes under a minute.

How many past papers should I do?

Do as many as you can mark carefully. A few papers where you study every mistake teach more than many papers you only skim.
