How to Write WAEC Essay Answers That Score High Marks

Examiners award marks for structure and clarity. Learn the pattern and your essays improve at once.

Michael Joseph
·📅 9 November 2026·⏱ 6 min read

Many strong students lose marks in English essays, not because they have nothing to say, but because the essay is disorganised, off the point or full of small errors. Examiners read many scripts. A clear plan, tidy paragraphs and correct English make their job easy, and that usually helps your score. Here is a method you can practise from today.

Know what the examiner is looking for

Marking schemes differ over time, so check the current WAEC English Language syllabus and past marking guidance. In general, essay marks reward four things:

  • Content: Did you answer the question and develop your ideas with relevant detail?
  • Organisation: Is there a clear beginning, middle and end, with sensible paragraphs?
  • Expression: Are your sentences clear and your vocabulary suitable?
  • Mechanical accuracy: Are spelling, punctuation, tenses and concord correct?

Notice that none of these requires fancy language. All of them can be trained.

Plan before you write

Spend about three to five minutes on a plan. Read the topic twice. Underline the key words: the type of essay, the audience and the focus. Then list three or four main points with one example each. Number them in the order you will use them.

1Read the topic twice and underline key words
2Identify the essay type and audience
3List three or four points with an example each
4Order the points
5Write the introduction last in your head, first on paper

A plan prevents the most common essay problem: rambling. It also stops you from running out of ideas in the middle of the third paragraph.

The structure that works

A standard essay has an introduction, body paragraphs and a conclusion. In a typical argumentative or expository piece, it looks like this.

PartWhat it doesRough size
IntroductionIntroduces the topic and states your position or direction3 to 5 sentences
Body paragraph 1 to 3One main idea each, with explanation and an example5 to 8 sentences each
ConclusionSums up and leaves a final thought, with no new points2 to 4 sentences

For each body paragraph, begin with a topic sentence that states the idea. Follow with explanation, then an example from Nigerian life: a market, a school, a community, a family. Real examples make your writing feel alive. End the paragraph by linking back to the topic.

Use linking words to guide the reader: firstly, in addition, however, as a result, in conclusion. Do not overuse them, but a few well-placed ones show the logic of your argument.

Different essay types, different rules

  • Narrative: tell a story in time order, with a clear beginning, a problem, a climax and an ending. Use past tense and keep it consistent.
  • Descriptive: paint a picture with the five senses. Avoid simply listing. Use specific details such as the smell of roasted corn or the noise of the generator.
  • Argumentative: take a side, give reasons, answer the opposite view briefly and conclude firmly.
  • Expository: explain a topic clearly with facts and examples, usually without taking sides.
  • Formal letter: follow the set layout (addresses, date, salutation, subject line, body, ending). Check the layout in your syllabus or textbook and keep to a polite, formal tone.
⚠️
Do not mix formats

An informal letter to a friend is written differently from a letter to a principal or an editor. Using slang or contractions in a formal letter loses marks, so check the audience in the question first.

Language habits that raise your mark

Write sentences you can control. A mix of short and medium sentences is better than one long sentence with six commas. Keep your tenses consistent. Match verbs with their subjects. Use capital letters, full stops and commas properly, and begin each new paragraph on a new line.

Weak sentence

  • The government should do something about it and also people should help and many things will improve
  • Nowadays, youths are always on phone and they do not read book
  • Corruption is a big problem which have destroyed our country

Stronger sentence

  • The government should repair the roads, and communities should help to maintain them
  • Many young people spend hours on their phones and read very few books
  • Corruption is a serious problem that has harmed our country

Be careful with common slips such as "alot", "infact", "I will be going" for "I am going" in the wrong context, and mixing "their" with "there". Reading your essay aloud in your head reveals many of them.

A worked outline

QuestionTopic: Write an article for your school magazine on the effects of social media on students. Outline the plan.
  1. Type and audience: article, readers are students and teachers
  2. Introduction: define social media, state that it has both gains and risks
  3. Point 1: gains such as access to information and group learning
  4. Point 2: risks such as distraction and late nights
  5. Point 3: advice on balanced use
  6. Conclusion: sum up and appeal for responsible use
AnswerA clear article with a catchy title, an introduction, three body paragraphs and a short closing appeal. Each paragraph has one idea and one example.

Use your last five minutes well

Keep five minutes to proofread. Check the title, spelling of key words, tenses, punctuation and whether every paragraph relates to the topic. Cross out mistakes neatly with a single line. Careless errors can cost a candidate a grade, so this time is worth it.

What to do today

  • Pick one past WAEC essay topic and plan it in five minutes
  • Write the essay in the time you would have in the exam
  • Check it against content, structure, vocabulary and accuracy
  • Note your three most common errors
  • Ask a teacher or friend to read it and comment

Frequently asked questions

How long should a WAEC English essay be?

Check the current WAEC English syllabus and the instruction on your paper for the expected length. Whatever the number, quality matters more than padding.

Which essay type should I choose?

Choose the one you can plan quickly and write about with real examples. Many candidates do well with narrative or letter writing, but there is no best type for everyone.

Do I lose marks for a poor introduction?

A weak start does not help. A clear, direct first paragraph shows the examiner your topic and direction, and it sets a good tone for the rest of the essay.

Should I use big words to impress the examiner?

No. Use words you can spell and use correctly. Simple, accurate English reads better than risky vocabulary.

How do I improve between now and the exam?

Write one timed essay a week, then check it against a checklist for content, structure, vocabulary and mechanical accuracy. Ask a teacher to read at least one.
