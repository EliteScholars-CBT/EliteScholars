How to Choose the Right JAMB Subject Combination for Your Course

The wrong combination can disqualify you before your score is even looked at. Check this before you register.

Why this decision matters

You can score very high in JAMB and still be refused a course because you wrote the wrong subjects. Admission bodies look at your subject combination before they look at your score. If the combination does not match what the course requires, the score cannot save you.

It is one of the few mistakes in the whole process that you can completely prevent. It takes an hour of careful checking before you register, and it can protect a whole year.

How the combination works

As a general structure, every candidate writes Use of English, then three other subjects chosen to suit the course. Those three subjects are not random. Each course has a required set, and sometimes a short list of alternatives.

Think of it as the course telling you which subjects prove you can handle it. Medicine expects strong science subjects. Law expects strong language and arts subjects. Accounting expects subjects around numbers and commerce. The logic is usually easy to see once you look at what the course demands.

🔑
Always verify the official source

Use the current JAMB brochure and syllabus, plus your target school's own admission page. Combinations are updated from time to time, so last year's advice from a friend may be out of date.

Examples of how combinations follow the course

These examples show the pattern. They are illustrations, so confirm the exact requirement for your own course and school before you decide.

  • Medicine and Surgery: English plus Biology, Chemistry and Physics.
  • Law: English plus Literature in English, Government or CRS or IRS, and Economics. Many schools list slightly different arts or social science options.
  • Accounting: English plus Mathematics, Economics and one more, often Commerce, Government or Accounting where applicable.
  • Computer Science: English plus Mathematics, Physics and one more science or a related subject such as Chemistry or Economics, depending on the school.
  • Mass Communication: English plus Literature in English, Government or Economics, and one other arts or social science subject.

Notice how the course name points to the subjects. Use that logic to double-check anything you are told.

A worked example: checking a combination step by step

QuestionAda wants to study Pharmacy. She is good in Biology, Chemistry and Mathematics, and plans to take Use of English, Biology, Chemistry and Mathematics. What should she do before registering?
  1. Find Pharmacy in the official JAMB brochure and note the listed subjects
  2. Compare the listed subjects with her plan: most Pharmacy listings use Chemistry plus Biology and Physics or Mathematics, but this must be verified
  3. Check the admission page of her target school for any extra rules
  4. If the school requires Physics, change her third or fourth subject before registering
  5. Write down the final combination and show it to a teacher
AnswerRegister only the combination that matches both JAMB and the school requirements

The point is the process, not the particular subjects. Ada's plan looked sensible, but she would not know whether it was acceptable until she checked two sources.

Check the school as well as JAMB

JAMB sets the national guide, but each school can add its own conditions. Some require specific O-level subjects at credit level. Some prefer certain combinations for competitive courses. Some have different lists for direct entry candidates.

So make a small table for each course and school you are considering, with the required UTME subjects, the required O-level credits and any other conditions. It takes time, but it clears up confusion later.

Risky approach

  • Ask a friend what they wrote
  • Let a registration centre choose for you
  • Pick subjects only because they seem easy
  • Change nothing after registration

Safe approach

  • Read the brochure and school requirements yourself
  • Confirm the choice before you pay and submit
  • Pick subjects that match the course and that you can score well in
  • Print or screenshot your slip and check every detail

Balancing what you need with what you can score

Sometimes a course lists a few options for the third or fourth subject. In that case, choose the option you are strongest in, or the one that overlaps most with what you are already studying for WAEC or NECO. Overlap saves study time.

Do not choose a course only because the subjects are easy. Your choice should fit what you actually want to do for several years. If you hesitate, talk to a teacher, a career counsellor or someone who has studied the course. If your first choice looks out of reach, ask about related courses. The same combination often opens more than one door.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting registration centre staff without checking yourself.
  • Selecting a course and subjects in a hurry on the last days.
  • Forgetting that O-level results also need to match the course.
  • Not saving a copy of your registration details.
  • Assuming the combination for a course is the same at every school.

What to do today

Do this before you pay for anything.

  • List your top three course choices
  • Look up each one in the official JAMB brochure
  • Visit each school's official admission page and note extra conditions
  • Compare required subjects with the ones you can score in
  • Ask a teacher to review your final choice
  • Save the final combination in your phone notes

Frequently asked questions

How many subjects do I write in JAMB?

Four: Use of English plus three subjects that fit your course. Confirm the current format on the official JAMB website.

Where do I find the correct subject combination for my course?

In the official JAMB brochure and syllabus for the current year, and in the admission requirements of your target school. Do not rely only on friends or cafe operators.

What happens if I register the wrong subjects?

You may be unable to be considered for the course. Rules about corrections change, so check the official JAMB portal or visit an accredited centre early rather than hoping to fix it later.

Can I change my course or subjects after registration?

Sometimes, within the correction rules and window JAMB sets. Check the current procedure on the official portal as soon as you notice a problem.

Do different universities require different combinations for the same course?

Sometimes they do. JAMB publishes a standard guide, but a school may have extra requirements, such as specific O-level grades. Check both.
