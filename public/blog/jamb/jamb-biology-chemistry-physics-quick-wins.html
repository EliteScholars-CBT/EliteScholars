JAMB Sciences: Quick Wins in Biology, Chemistry and Physics

Each JAMB science has predictable high-yield areas. Here is where to focus in Biology, Chemistry and Physics, with worked examples you can copy.

Start With What the Syllabus Rewards

The JAMB sciences are not random. Each subject follows an official syllabus, and the examiners come back to the same families of ideas year after year. That gives you a clear strategy: learn the repeating areas first, then fill gaps.

Always check the current JAMB syllabus for your subject so you know what is included. Then use past questions to see where the questions come from. The sections below describe the kinds of areas that tend to be rewarding, and how to practise them. Treat them as a starting order, not a guarantee.

Biology: Learn Processes and Draw Diagrams

Biology rewards students who can connect structure to function. Instead of memorising isolated facts, study each topic as a story: what goes in, what happens, what comes out.

  • Cell biology and transport. Know the parts of the cell, the difference between diffusion, osmosis and active transport, and what each one needs.
  • Nutrition, respiration and photosynthesis. Learn the word equations and where each process happens.
  • Genetics. Practise monohybrid crosses with a Punnett square. These are a steady source of marks.
  • Ecology. Food chains, food webs, energy flow and the effects of human activity.
  • Systems of the body. Digestion, circulation, excretion and the nervous system: learn the organs and their roles.
QuestionA heterozygous tall pea plant (Tt) is crossed with a short plant (tt). What fraction of the offspring is expected to be tall? Tall is dominant
  1. List the gametes: Tt gives T or t
  2. tt gives only t
  3. Combine them: Tt, tt, Tt, tt
  4. Count the tall plants: those with at least one T are Tt and Tt
AnswerHalf, or 1/2, of the offspring are expected to be tall

Common mistake: writing a definition that is almost right. Biology options are often close to each other, so learn the exact wording of key terms such as habitat, population and community.

Chemistry: Master the Mole and Its Neighbours

Chemistry is easier than it looks once you realise that many calculation questions run on one idea: the mole. If you are confident here, a lot of marks follow.

Number of molesn = m / Mn is the number of moles, m is the mass in grams and M is the molar mass in g/mol

Other areas worth prioritising:

  • Atomic structure and the periodic table. Electron configuration and trends across a period and down a group.
  • Bonding. Ionic, covalent and metallic, with simple properties of each.
  • Acids, bases and salts. Definitions, indicators, neutralisation and the general reactions.
  • Organic chemistry. Names, homologous series and simple reactions of alkanes, alkenes and alcohols.
  • Electrolysis and rates of reaction. Often tested with short conceptual questions.
QuestionHow many moles are in 11.7 g of sodium chloride, NaCl? Use atomic masses Na = 23 and Cl = 35.5
  1. Find the molar mass: 23 + 35.5 = 58.5 g/mol
  2. Write the formula: n = m / M
  3. Substitute: n = 11.7 / 58.5
Answern = 0.2 mol

Always state which atomic masses you are using, and carry units through every step. If the question gives the masses, use exactly those.

Physics: Pick the Right Equation

Physics questions often fail not because students cannot calculate, but because they pick the wrong formula. A good habit is to write down what is given, what is wanted and then choose the formula that links them.

  • Motion. The equations of uniform acceleration are heavily practised: v = u + at, s = ut + 1/2 at2 and v2 = u2 + 2as.
  • Forces and Newton's laws. F = ma, weight, friction and moments.
  • Work, energy and power. Kinetic and potential energy and their conversions.
  • Electricity. Ohm's law, series and parallel resistors, and power.
  • Waves, light and heat. Many questions here are short and conceptual, so they are quick marks.
QuestionA stone is dropped from rest and falls 45 m. Taking g = 10 m/s2, what is its speed just before it hits the ground?
  1. List the given: u = 0, a = 10, s = 45
  2. Choose the formula that has no time: v2 = u2 + 2as
  3. Substitute: v2 = 0 + 2 x 10 x 45 = 900
  4. Take the square root
Answerv = 30 m/s

Check units before you finish. Convert minutes to seconds and kilometres to metres where needed.

The Order That Pays Off

You will not master every topic. Use this order to get the best return on your time.

  1. Calculation patterns firstMole calculations, motion equations and Ohm's law repeat often, so master them early
  2. Short conceptual topics nextDefinitions and classifications are quick to revise and quick to answer
  3. Diagrams and processesDraw them from memory until you stop making mistakes
  4. Past questions by topicDo ten questions per topic, then review every error
  5. Mixed timed sets lastPractise switching topics under time pressure

Mistakes That Cost Marks

  • Skipping units or using the wrong ones.
  • Choosing an option that is true in general but does not answer the question asked.
  • Not reading words like NOT, EXCEPT and ONLY.
  • Memorising answers to past questions without understanding the idea. Examiners reword questions.
  • Leaving a topic because it looks hard. Many hard-looking topics have only a few core ideas.
💡
Make a one-page formula sheet

Write the formulas for each subject on a single page. Re-write it from memory every few days. By exam week it will be in your head.

What to Do Today

  • Pick one science and one topic from the lists above
  • Do ten past questions on that topic before reading your notes
  • Write down every formula you used from memory
  • Redo the mole, motion or genetics example above without looking
  • Note your three most common mistakes
  • Plan tomorrow's topic

Frequently asked questions

Which JAMB science is easiest to score high in?

It depends on your strengths. Biology rewards memory and understanding of diagrams, Chemistry rewards formulas plus mole calculations, and Physics rewards practice with equations. Use past questions to see which you improve in fastest.

How should I study JAMB Biology?

Learn the syllabus topics by themes: cells and processes, ecology, genetics and the body systems. Then test yourself with past questions and draw the key diagrams from memory.

How do I master JAMB Chemistry calculations?

Memorise the mole formulas, practise balancing equations and always write units. Calculation questions repeat in a small number of patterns.

Is JAMB Physics mostly formulas?

A large part is, but you also need to understand what each symbol means and to pick the right formula. Practise identifying the given and unknown values first.

How many past questions should I do?

Quality matters more than a count. Do each question, review every error, and redo the ones you missed until you understand the reason.
