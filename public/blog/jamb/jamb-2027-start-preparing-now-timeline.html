JAMB 2027: Why Starting in October Beats Starting in January (14-Week Plan)

Most candidates only panic when registration opens. Here is a simple 14-week plan that puts you months ahead before the crowd even begins.

Why the early start wins

Every year the same story repeats. A candidate hears that registration is open, panics, buys three textbooks and tries to read everything in a few weeks. The exam is a Computer-Based Test of objective questions across four subjects, and it rewards a wide, steady knowledge base. You cannot build that in a rush.

The early starter has a different experience. By the time the crowd begins, they have already met every topic at least once and have a list of their weak areas. Their last weeks are for sharpening, not learning from zero. That difference is worth far more marks than any last-minute trick.

🔑
The real advantage

Memory needs repetition over time. Seeing a topic three times across three months sticks far better than seeing it three times in one week.

Step zero: fix your four subjects and your target

Before any timetable, settle two things. First, your subject combination: English plus three subjects that match your course. Confirm this on the official JAMB brochure for your course, because a wrong combination causes problems later. Second, a target score and the course you want. Check the official school and JAMB portals for current cut-off information rather than relying on rumours from friends.

Write your four subjects on a sheet of paper and give each one a simple rating: strong, average or weak. Be honest. This sheet drives the rest of the plan.

The 14-week plan

Fourteen weeks is about three and a half months. It is long enough to cover the syllabus properly and short enough to keep you serious. Adjust the week count to your own situation, but keep the order of the phases.

  1. Weeks 1 to 2Collect the official syllabus for each subject and read the recommended textbooks list
  2. Weeks 3 to 8Study topic by topic, one subject focus per day, with 10 questions after each topic
  3. Weeks 9 to 11Take full past-question sets by subject and mark yourself honestly
  4. Weeks 12 to 13Repair weak topics found in your marking and redo the questions you missed
  5. Week 14Timed CBT practice across all four subjects and a calm review

Weeks 1 to 2: set up

Get the current official JAMB syllabus for each of your subjects. It lists the exact topics that can be examined, so you stop wasting time on things that are not in it. Turn each syllabus into a checklist. You will tick off topics as you finish them.

Weeks 3 to 8: learn and test

This is the longest phase. Study one topic, then answer around ten objective questions on it the same day. Do not wait until the end of the month to test yourself. A question you get wrong on the day you studied the topic teaches you far more than one you meet weeks later.

Weeks 9 to 11: past questions as diagnosis

Now work through past questions in blocks, and mark them. For each wrong answer write the topic and the reason: did you not know it, misread it, or run out of time? Patterns appear quickly. Many candidates discover that most of their losses come from three or four topics.

Weeks 12 to 14: repair and rehearse

Go back to those weak topics and study them again from the textbook, then retest. In the final week, do timed practice using a CBT-style environment, ideally on a phone or laptop, so the screen feels normal on exam day.

A weekly rhythm you can actually keep

Here is a sample for a student in school during the day. Change it to fit your life.

  • Monday to Thursday: 90 minutes in the evening, one subject per night, with Use of English on two of the four nights since everyone writes it.
  • Friday: 30 minutes of light revision of the week's notes.
  • Saturday: two blocks of 90 minutes, one for your weakest subject and one for past questions.
  • Sunday: rest, then 30 minutes to plan the coming week.

That comes to roughly 10 to 11 hours a week. Over 14 weeks it is about 150 hours of focused work, which is a very different position from the candidate who starts late.

A worked example: turning a weak area into marks

Suppose your first set of Mathematics questions shows you missing most number base questions. Do not just reread the chapter. Break the problem into small rules and practise each one.

QuestionConvert 101101 in base 2 to base 10.
  1. Write the place values from the right: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32
  2. Match each digit of 101101 to a place value, starting from the right: 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1
  3. Keep only the places that have a 1: 1 + 4 + 8 + 32
  4. Add them together
Answer45

Once you can do this without thinking, add the reverse conversion, then addition in other bases. Three small skills practised on three days beat one long panicked evening.

Common mistakes in early preparation

Common mistake

  • Reading notes for hours without testing yourself
  • Studying only your favourite subject
  • Skipping English because you speak it every day
  • Changing the plan every week

Better way

  • Ten questions after every topic
  • Giving the most time to your weakest subject
  • Treating English as a full subject with its own practice
  • Keeping one plan for at least a month before adjusting it

Also avoid buying pirated answer packages or trusting anyone who promises to change your score. Your marks come from your own preparation, and the practice you do now is the only thing that carries into the exam hall.

What to do today

You do not need to feel ready to begin. You only need a first small step today. Then repeat it tomorrow.

  • Write your four subjects on one sheet and rate each strong, average or weak
  • Download or copy the current official syllabus for each subject
  • Pick the start date of week 1 and write it on your wall
  • Study one topic tonight and answer ten questions on it
  • Block your weekly study times in your phone calendar

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing for JAMB 2027?

Now. Even if you are still in SS3, a few focused hours a week on your four subjects is enough to build a lead. Waiting for registration to open usually leaves you with only a few weeks to cover two years of work.

How many hours a day should I study for JAMB?

Two focused hours on a school day and three to four on weekends is realistic for most students and is better than a plan you abandon after a week. Quality and consistency matter more than the headline number.

Do I need to finish the whole syllabus before I start past questions?

No. Start past questions early on topics you have covered, because they show you how JAMB actually asks things. Use them to decide what to study next, not as a final test.

What if I am writing WAEC or NECO at the same time?

Your WAEC or NECO preparation overlaps heavily with JAMB, since both follow similar syllabuses. Study a topic once for both, then practise JAMB-style objective questions on it.
