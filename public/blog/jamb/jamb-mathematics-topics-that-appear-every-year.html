JAMB Mathematics: The Topics That Appear Almost Every Year (and How to Study Them)

You cannot study the whole of Maths in the time you have. Focus on these high-frequency areas first.

Why a topic list helps

The Mathematics syllabus is wide, and the exam has limited time and questions. You cannot give each topic equal attention. Past papers show that some areas show up again and again, while others appear rarely. The smart approach is to master the frequent ones first, then widen your coverage.

Use this article as a starting map, not a guarantee. Always compare it with the current official JAMB syllabus and with your own results from past-question practice. The topics below are the ones that students most commonly meet, grouped by area.

Number and numeration

This is the foundation, and it tends to appear every year. Know number bases, fractions, decimals and percentages, surds, indices and logarithms, and ratio and proportion.

Number bases

You must be able to convert between bases and do simple addition and subtraction in them. The method is always place value.

QuestionConvert 45 in base 10 to base 2.
  1. Divide 45 by 2 repeatedly and record remainders: 45 gives 22 remainder 1
  2. 22 gives 11 remainder 0
  3. 11 gives 5 remainder 1
  4. 5 gives 2 remainder 1
  5. 2 gives 1 remainder 0
  6. 1 gives 0 remainder 1
  7. Read the remainders from bottom to top

Indices and logarithms

Learn the laws well: am x an = am+n and am / an = am-n. For logs, remember that loga b asks the question: to what power must a be raised to get b? So log2 8 = 3 and log3 9 = 2, which gives a sum of 5.

Algebra

Algebra carries many marks and links to almost everything else. Focus on:

  • Solving linear and quadratic equations, by factorising, and by formula when factorising fails.
  • Simultaneous equations by elimination or substitution.
  • Variation: direct, inverse, joint and partial.
  • Changing the subject of a formula.
  • Inequalities and simple graphs.
QuestionSolve x2 - 5x + 6 = 0.
  1. Look for two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5: these are -2 and -3
  2. Factorise: (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0
  3. Set each bracket to zero
Answerx = 2 or x = 3

Option-testing shortcut

In an objective test you can often substitute the four options into the equation. Test the simplest option first. It is not a replacement for learning the method, but it rescues you when you are stuck.

Geometry, mensuration and trigonometry

Geometry questions reward knowing a set of facts. Learn angle properties of triangles and polygons, circle theorems, similar triangles and the formulas for area and volume of common shapes.

In trigonometry, know the three ratios and the special angles for 30, 45 and 60 degrees. Practise bearings and angles of elevation and depression, because word problems on them are common. Always draw a diagram first.

Sine, cosine, tangentsin = opposite / hypotenuse ; cos = adjacent / hypotenuse ; tan = opposite / adjacentUse the right-angled triangle and label sides from the angle you are working with

Statistics and probability

These topics are often easy marks if you know the definitions. Practise mean, median and mode from a list and from a frequency table. Learn to read bar charts, pie charts and histograms. For probability, remember that probability equals the number of favourable outcomes divided by the total outcomes, and that all probabilities of a situation add up to 1.

Careless errors cost marks here more than difficulty does. When finding a median, order the numbers first. When finding a mean from a frequency table, multiply each value by its frequency before adding.

A study order that makes sense

Start with what supports everything else, then move outward.

1Number work and indices
2Algebra and equations
3Geometry and mensuration
4Trigonometry
5Statistics and probability
6Mixed timed sets

Spend about two weeks on the first two if you are weak, and a week each on the rest. After each topic, answer 15 to 20 past questions and keep an error log, as explained in our past-questions guide.

Habits that raise your Maths score

  • Write your working: Even in a CBT you can use paper. Mental maths under time pressure creates slips.
  • Check the units and what is asked: Many wrong answers are correct calculations that answer a different question.
  • Do not get stuck: If a question takes more than a minute, flag it and return.
  • Practise daily: Maths is a skill. Twenty minutes every day beats three hours once a week.

What to do today

Choose the topic on this list where you feel least confident and give it one full session.

  • Get the current official JAMB Maths syllabus and tick the topics you know
  • Pick your weakest topic from this list
  • Learn its rules and solve five examples by hand
  • Answer ten past questions on it and log your errors
  • Plan which topic you will study tomorrow

Frequently asked questions

Which JAMB Mathematics topics are most important?

Number and numeration, algebra, geometry and mensuration, trigonometry, and statistics are heavily tested. Check the official syllabus and your own past-question practice to see which areas appear most often.

Can I use a calculator in JAMB Maths?

Calculator rules are set by JAMB and can change. Check the current official guidelines. In either case, practise manual methods so you are not stuck.

How do I get faster at JAMB Maths?

Learn the shortcut for each topic, such as substituting options into an equation, and practise timed sets. Speed comes from recognising question types.

I am weak in Maths. Is it too late?

No. Start with number work, basic algebra and indices, since they support everything else. Steady daily practice can lift a weak score noticeably.
