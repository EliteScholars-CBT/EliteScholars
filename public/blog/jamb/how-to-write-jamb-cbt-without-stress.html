JAMB CBT Day: What to Do the Night Before and the Morning Of

Small decisions on the day before your JAMB CBT can add or remove marks. Use this night-before and morning-of plan, plus a simple method for managing time inside the hall.

The Day Before: Fix Logistics, Not Knowledge

The day before your CBT is not the time to learn new topics. What you can still change is your readiness: where you will be, what you carry, and how rested you are. Many candidates lose focus on the day because of avoidable problems like a missing slip, a wrong centre address or a long, unplanned trip.

Do these in the afternoon, while you still have energy:

  1. Print or find your exam slip and read every detail: centre name, address, date and time.
  2. Visit or map the route. If the centre is far, know the park to stop at, the transport you will use and how long it takes at the hour you will travel.
  3. Pack your bag. Slip, identification as required by your slip, a pen in case it is needed, water and a light snack. Check the instructions on your slip for what is and is not allowed.
  4. Prepare your clothes and phone charger, and set two alarms.

Check the rules on your slip

Items allowed or banned in the hall can change. Read your own slip and JAMB's current instructions rather than relying on what friends tell you.

The Night Before: Light Revision and Real Sleep

Spend one or two hours at most on revision. Focus on things that are short and high value: formulas, definitions, a list of common mistakes you have made in practice, and one or two topics you have been mixing up.

Then stop. Studying late into the night trades sleep for knowledge you will not be able to use. Tired brains misread questions and make careless errors. Aim for seven to eight hours of sleep.

  • Eat a normal dinner. Do not try a new meal tonight.
  • Switch off notifications and avoid exam gist on social media, which spreads fear faster than facts.
  • If your mind races, write tomorrow's plan on paper. Writing it down lets your brain let go.

The Morning Of: Arrive Calm, Not Early and Frantic

Wake up with enough time not to rush. Eat a light breakfast with something steady like bread and egg, or oats, and drink water. Avoid heavy, oily food and avoid too much caffeine if you are already nervous.

Leave early enough to absorb delays. Arriving with time to spare lets you settle, use the toilet, and go through registration without panic.

At the centre, avoid group debates about hard questions. Someone will always say something that sounds frightening. You cannot change the paper now, so protect your focus. A quick look at your formula sheet is fine. Beyond that, breathe, walk slowly and stay quiet.

Inside the Hall: A Simple Time Plan

The CBT is a race against the clock, so time per question is the skill that matters most. First, check on your slip and on screen how much time you have and how many questions you will face. Then do the arithmetic.

QuestionSuppose you have 120 minutes for 180 questions. How long can you spend per question if you want 10 minutes left for review? Use your own exam's figures on the day
  1. Subtract review time from total time: 120 - 10 = 110 minutes
  2. Divide by the number of questions: 110 / 180 = about 0.61 minutes
  3. Convert to seconds: 0.61 x 60 = about 37 seconds per question
  4. Plan checkpoints: for example, about 20 percent of the questions done after 20 percent of the working time
AnswerAbout 37 seconds per question on average. Short calculation or recall questions should take less, so you have extra time for the harder ones

This is only an example, since the number of questions and the time depend on the subjects and format. The method is what matters: know your pace and check it at checkpoints.

Practical habits that save marks:

  • Answer something for every question. Do not leave blanks. Eliminate options you know are wrong, then choose the best of the rest.
  • Flag and move. If a question holds you for more than a minute, flag it, pick a provisional answer, and continue.
  • Read the question twice. Words like NOT, EXCEPT and LEAST change the answer.
  • Do calculation questions with care. Write the working on rough paper if it is allowed, and check units.
  • Start with your strongest subject if the system lets you choose, then use the time left for the harder ones.

Handling Nerves and Technical Hiccups

If your mind goes blank, try this. Stop and take five slow breaths: in for four counts, out for six. Then find any question you can answer and answer it. A couple of quick successes break the spiral.

If the computer freezes or something looks wrong, raise your hand and tell the invigilator right away. Do not wait. Do not try to fix it yourself.

Remember that everyone in the hall is nervous. The calmest candidate is usually the one who prepared a routine in advance.

🔑
Practice the exact conditions

The best preparation for a CBT is doing timed computer-based practice before the day. If you have used past-question quizzes under a timer, the real hall will feel more familiar.

What to Do Today

  • Read your exam slip and write down the centre address and time
  • Plan your route and transport
  • Pack slip and ID and water
  • Revise formulas and weak points for one hour only
  • Eat dinner and switch off by a fixed time
  • Set two alarms

Frequently asked questions

What should I do the night before JAMB?

Do light revision of formulas and weak points only, confirm your slip and centre, pack your documents, and sleep. Do not start new topics.

What should I bring to the JAMB CBT centre?

Bring what JAMB tells you to bring, which normally includes your exam slip and a valid identification as stated on the slip. Always check the instructions on your own slip and the official JAMB portal.

How do I manage time in the JAMB CBT?

Divide the total time by the number of questions to get a pace per question, plan a few minutes at the end for review, and flag hard questions to return to instead of getting stuck.

Should I guess if I do not know the answer?

Yes. Do not leave questions unanswered. After eliminating options you know are wrong, choose the best remaining one. Confirm the marking rules in JAMB's current guidelines.

How do I calm down when I panic in the hall?

Pause, breathe slowly for a minute, and start with a question you can answer. A few quick wins settle your mind and give you momentum.
