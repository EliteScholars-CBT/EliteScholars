How to Score 300+ in JAMB 2027: The Exact Study Plan That Actually Works

Scoring 300+ in JAMB is not about being naturally gifted. It is about having the right system and sticking to it. Here is the exact breakdown, from what to study to how to think in the exam hall.

Michael Joseph
·📅 27 April 2026·⏱ 11 min read

First, Let Us Kill the Myth

Every year after JAMB results drop, you see the same TikTok videos. Someone scores 320 and everyone comments "she must be a genius." Someone else scores 180 and their relatives whisper about intelligence. Both narratives are wrong, and both are dangerous.

Scoring 300+ in JAMB is not a function of raw intelligence. It is a function of deliberate preparation, strategic subject knowledge, and the ability to execute under time pressure. The exam has 180 questions spread across four subjects. You have 2 hours and 30 minutes — 150 minutes total. That works out to roughly 50 seconds per question. The candidates who score 300+ are not smarter than you. They have simply drilled this format until it feels automatic.

Here is the complete system.

Start With the JAMB Syllabus, Not Textbooks

This is the number one mistake Nigerian students make. They pick up a thick SS3 textbook and start reading from page one as if they are preparing for university finals. The JAMB syllabus is a specific document that outlines exactly what JAMB can and cannot test you on. Topics not in the syllabus will not appear in your exam. Topics that are in the syllabus will appear — often repeatedly across multiple years.

Download the JAMB syllabus for every subject you are sitting. It is free on the JAMB portal. Print it out. Highlight every topic. Your entire study effort should be organised around this document. If a topic is not on the syllabus, deprioritise it ruthlessly. Your study time is finite, and JAMB is testing a finite body of knowledge.

The Subject Breakdown That Actually Matters

Every JAMB candidate sits Use of English compulsorily. Then you add three elective subjects depending on your course of study. Let us break down how to approach each strategically.

Use of English is 60 questions — exactly one-third of your entire exam. This is not the subject to treat casually because you speak English every day. The JAMB English questions test very specific things: comprehension passages, summary, oral English, vocabulary in context, and grammar rules. Past question analysis shows that certain question types repeat almost every year. Antonyms and synonyms in context, figure of speech identification, and comprehension are perennial favourites. Practice past questions specifically — not general English reading.

Mathematics rewards candidates who understand concepts rather than memorise procedures. The JAMB Maths syllabus covers algebra, trigonometry, statistics, coordinate geometry, calculus basics, and number theory. The questions are typically not as deep as WAEC Maths — they test whether you understand the concept and can apply it quickly. Focus on topics with the highest frequency in past questions: quadratic equations, indices, logarithms, sequences and series, mensuration, and statistics. Use a simple calculator — JAMB allows basic calculators for Maths and science subjects.

Physics requires conceptual clarity more than mathematical depth. You need to understand the principles and be able to apply them to situations you may not have seen before. High-frequency JAMB Physics topics include mechanics (motion, force, energy, momentum), waves, electricity and magnetism, optics, and modern physics. Do not just memorise formulas — understand what each formula means physically. When you understand why F = ma, you can answer novel questions about force and motion without ever having seen that specific question before.

Chemistry is perhaps the most memorisation-heavy of the sciences at JAMB level, but it is also very consistent in what it tests. Periodicity, chemical bonding, reactions of organic compounds, electrochemistry, and mole calculations appear in almost every year's paper. The organic chemistry questions — naming compounds, identifying functional groups, predicting reaction products — are predictable once you master the patterns.

Biology, Economics, Government, Literature, and Accounting each follow their own patterns that a thorough past question analysis will reveal. The principle is the same across all: study the syllabus topics, use past questions to identify what actually gets tested, and concentrate your effort accordingly.

The 6-Month Study Plan Structure

If your target exam date is April 2027, you have roughly six months of meaningful preparation time from October 2026. Here is how to structure it without burning out or wasting time.

Months one and two are for foundation building. Go through each subject's syllabus topics systematically. Do not rush. Understand each topic before moving to the next. Use your school textbooks for content, then immediately test yourself with past questions on that specific topic. Do not wait until you have studied everything before you start practicing questions — practice starts on day one, topic by topic.

Months three and four shift to intensive past question drilling. JAMB has been conducting UTME since 2010 in its current format. That is 15-plus years of papers. Work through them systematically, noting which topics come up most frequently, which question types confuse you, and which you answer correctly and quickly. Build a personal "weak topic" list and revisit those areas with targeted study.

Month five is for timed mock exams. Simulate the actual exam conditions: 180 questions, 150 minutes, no distractions. You can use CBT practice platforms — the EliteScholars CBT app is built exactly for this — to experience the timed format repeatedly. Your score on practice tests will rise as you build speed and confidence. Target completing at least 20 full mock exams in this month.

Month six — the final month — is for consolidation and mental preparation. Do a few mock exams weekly to stay sharp, but do not cram new topics. Review your weak areas, sleep properly, and prepare your logistics for exam day. Know the location of your CBT centre. Visit it physically if possible. Plan your transport. Remove uncertainty about everything you can control.

The CBT Exam Hall Mindset

Understanding the format of JAMB's computer-based test is as important as subject knowledge. The questions appear on screen one at a time. You can flag questions and return to them. The system tracks your remaining time prominently. This is both useful and psychologically dangerous — watching the clock tick down causes many candidates to panic and make careless errors on questions they actually know the answer to.

Develop a personal pacing strategy. A simple one: allocate your 150 minutes across subjects roughly by question count. English gets about 50 minutes for its 60 questions. Each other subject gets about 33 minutes for its 40 questions. Within each subject, answer the questions you know immediately and flag the ones you are unsure about. Return to flagged questions in the time remaining rather than getting stuck on one question for five minutes.

Eliminate wrong answers. JAMB questions typically have four options. Even if you do not know the answer for certain, you can usually eliminate one or two clearly wrong options. Choosing between two options gives you a 50 percent chance on a guess — much better than random selection from four. Educated guessing is a legitimate strategy for questions you are genuinely unsure about, because there is no penalty for wrong answers in JAMB.

Read every question completely before looking at the options. This sounds obvious but under exam pressure, many candidates read half a question, think they know what is being asked, and select an answer that is wrong because they misread the question. Slow down for 3 seconds on each question to ensure you understand what is actually being asked.

The Resources That Actually Help

You do not need to spend a lot of money to prepare well for JAMB. The JAMB syllabus is free. Your school textbooks are sufficient for content. JAMB past questions are available for free online and through apps designed for this purpose. What you need to invest is time — consistent, deliberate, daily time.

Consistency beats intensity every time in exam preparation. One hour every single day for six months produces better results than five hours every weekend with nothing in between. Your brain needs repeated exposure and sleep to consolidate what you have learned. Study regularly, sleep fully, and test yourself constantly.

The students who score 300 and above in JAMB are not the ones who started studying a month before the exam and pulled all-nighters. They are the ones who built a system, worked the system consistently, and trusted the process when it felt slow. Build your system now. Start tomorrow morning. Six months from now, your result will reflect it.

One Last Thing

Practice CBT specifically. JAMB is computer-based, and there is a skill to navigating that format efficiently — using the flag function, moving between questions, reading on a screen. If you have only ever studied from textbooks and paper, spending time on a CBT practice platform before your actual exam is not optional. It is essential. Your 300+ is not a fantasy. It is a plan. Now you have the plan.
