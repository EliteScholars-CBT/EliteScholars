How to Survive Your First Semester in a Nigerian University

Freshers' week is fun, but the first semester sets your CGPA foundation. Here is a week-by-week plan, a study routine and the traps to avoid.

Why the First Semester Matters

Your CGPA is an average across all your semesters. That means the early results shape the rest of the journey. A strong first semester gives you room to breathe later. A weak one forces you to chase marks for years.

There is also a habits effect. The routines you set in the first month tend to stay with you. If you attend, revise a little each day and ask questions, it becomes normal. If you drift, it also becomes normal.

This guide gives you a plan you can start in week one, even if you have already missed a few days.

Week One: Get Your Bearings

Orientation and registration are chaotic, but a few tasks cannot wait.

  • Complete registration and course registration. Make sure all your courses are on the system, with the correct units.
  • Get your timetable. Write it down and put it where you will see it.
  • Find your lecture halls. Walk the routes before the first class so you are not lost.
  • Collect handouts and course outlines. The outline tells you topics, assessment methods and sometimes the textbook list.
  • Join your class group chat, and add the notes and announcements to a single place.
💡
Learn how marks are split

For each course, find out how much is continuous assessment and how much is the final exam. That tells you where your effort pays most.

Build a Weekly Routine

A simple timetable beats good intentions. Block your lectures first, then add fixed study slots.

  1. Week 1Register, get the timetable and meet your coursemates
  2. Weeks 2 to 4Attend all lectures and review notes the same day
  3. Weeks 5 to 7Begin past-question practice and prepare for continuous assessment
  4. Weeks 8 to 10Revisit weak topics and form a small study group
  5. Weeks 11 and beyondFull revision, timed practice and exam logistics

Your own weeks will differ, because universities have different calendars. Ask what applies in your school.

A daily pattern that works for many freshers:

  1. After each lecture, spend 10 to 15 minutes tidying your notes while the class is fresh.
  2. Evening, do one focused study block of 45 to 60 minutes on a single course.
  3. Weekend, do a longer review and some past questions.

Know How Your CGPA Is Built

Understanding the numbers helps you plan. Many Nigerian universities use a 5-point scale, where A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and F = 0. Check your school's own grading scheme in the handbook.

Here is a sample first semester with six courses.

QuestionCompute the GPA. Units and grades: 3 units B, 2 units A, 3 units C, 2 units D, 3 units A, 2 units E
  1. Quality points = units x grade points: 3x4 = 12, 2x5 = 10, 3x3 = 9, 2x2 = 4, 3x5 = 15, 2x1 = 2
  2. Add the quality points: 12 + 10 + 9 + 4 + 15 + 2 = 52
  3. Add the units: 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 15
  4. Divide: 52 / 15
AnswerGPA = 3.47 (to two decimal places)

You can see how quickly a few small courses matter: the 2-unit D and E pulled the average down. Every unit counts, so do not write off courses that seem minor.

Study Habits That Work in University

University is different from secondary school. Lectures move fast, textbooks are thick, and nobody is checking your homework. Here are habits that carry you through.

  • Test yourself. Do not just re-read. Close your notes and write what you remember, or answer past questions.
  • Study what is taught. Lecturers set exams from their own lectures and handouts. Start there before outside material.
  • Ask early. If a topic is unclear, ask a coursemate, a senior student or the lecturer in the first week it arises.
  • Form a small group. Two to four serious students can quiz each other and explain tough topics.
  • Keep your phone away during study blocks.

What trips freshers

  • Cramming everything the week before exams
  • Copying a friend's notes without reading
  • Skipping lectures because they are many
  • Going silent when lost

What helps

  • Short daily review of notes
  • Making your own summaries and questions
  • Attending and asking questions
  • Asking a coursemate or lecturer early

Money, Friends and Wellbeing

Academic success is tied to the rest of your life, so do not ignore it.

  • Budget early. Write down what you receive and what you spend each week. Small daily spending adds up fast.
  • Choose friends carefully. Spend time with people who study, support you and respect your goals.
  • Sleep and eat. Poor sleep reduces memory and mood. Eating regularly helps you focus.
  • Seek help. If you feel overwhelmed, speak to a trusted senior, a relative or your school's counselling unit if available.
  • Stay safe. Be careful with strangers who offer shortcuts, "sorting" or easy grades. Genuine results come from your own work.

Mistakes to Avoid in Semester One

  • Treating the first weeks like a holiday.
  • Missing continuous assessments, which can be a large part of your grade.
  • Not collecting past questions until the last week.
  • Over-committing to clubs or side hustles.
  • Comparing your pace with everyone else. Focus on a steady, repeatable plan.

What to Do Today

  • Write your timetable and place it on your wall or phone
  • List each course with its units and how marks are split
  • Collect past questions for two courses
  • Set two study blocks for this week
  • Find one coursemate to study with
  • Save your lecturers' contact or class group details

Frequently asked questions

How important is the first semester for my CGPA?

Very. A strong first year gives you a cushion, while a weak start can be hard to lift later because your CGPA is an average over many courses.

How many hours should a fresher study each day?

There is no universal number, but a steady routine of two to four focused hours outside lectures works for many students. Consistency matters more than long marathons.

Should I join campus clubs as a fresher?

One or two clubs can help you grow and make friends, but protect your study time. Choose activities that add something useful and do not clash with lectures.

What if I fall behind in the first month?

Act early. Get the notes you missed, ask a coursemate or lecturer for help, and plan catch-up slots. Do not wait until exam week.

How can I avoid carry-overs?

Attend classes, submit assignments, do continuous assessments and practise past questions before exams. Carry-overs usually come from missed fundamentals, not one bad day.
