CGPA Explained: How to Calculate It and How to Improve It

Many students do not understand how CGPA is calculated until it is too late. Here is the full method on a 5.0 scale, with worked examples and a realistic plan to improve.

GPA and CGPA: The Difference

GPA is your Grade Point Average for a single semester. CGPA is your Cumulative Grade Point Average, which combines every semester you have completed. Your final CGPA decides your degree class, so it is worth understanding early.

Both are weighted by credit units. A 4-unit course matters more than a 1-unit course. That is why you cannot just average your letter grades.

The 5.0 Scale and Grade Points

Many Nigerian universities use a 5-point system. A common version is:

GradeTypical score rangeGrade point
A70 to 1005
B60 to 694
C50 to 593
D45 to 492
E40 to 441
F0 to 390

Score ranges can differ by school, and some schools drop grade E. Always follow the scheme in your own handbook.

CGPACGPA = total quality points / total credit unitsQuality points for a course = credit units x grade point. Add quality points and units across all semesters before dividing

Worked Example: One Semester

Take a first semester with six courses.

QuestionFind the GPA. Courses: 3 units B, 2 units A, 3 units C, 2 units D, 3 units A, 2 units E
  1. Quality points for each: 3 x 4 = 12
  2. 2 x 5 = 10
  3. 3 x 3 = 9
  4. 2 x 2 = 4
  5. 3 x 5 = 15
  6. 2 x 1 = 2
  7. Sum of quality points = 52 and total units = 15
  8. GPA = 52 / 15
AnswerGPA = 3.47

Worked Example: Moving to CGPA

Now add a second semester with 16 units. Suppose its total quality points come to 66 (so its GPA is 66 / 16 = 4.13). Notice you cannot get CGPA by averaging 3.47 and 4.13 blindly, because the semesters have different units.

QuestionWhat is the CGPA after both semesters? Semester 1: 52 points, 15 units. Semester 2: 66 points, 16 units
  1. Add the quality points: 52 + 66 = 118
  2. Add the units: 15 + 16 = 31
  3. Divide: 118 / 31
AnswerCGPA = 3.81

The simple average of 3.47 and 4.13 is 3.80. It happens to be close here, but with a big difference in units the two methods can differ a lot. Use the weighted method every time.

What Your CGPA Means

Degree classes depend on CGPA ranges, and these vary slightly by university. A widely used 5.0 scheme is:

  • First Class: 4.50 to 5.00
  • Second Class Upper: 3.50 to 4.49
  • Second Class Lower: 2.40 to 3.49
  • Third Class: 1.50 to 2.39

Check your school's regulations for the exact ranges, pass rules and what happens below the lower limit.

Projecting Next Semester

You can use the formula to see what is possible. Continue the example: you have 118 quality points over 31 units, and you will take 18 units next semester.

QuestionWhat CGPA do you reach if you score B in every course next semester? And what if you score A in every course?
  1. Quality points for all B: 18 x 4 = 72, so total = 118 + 72 = 190
  2. Total units = 31 + 18 = 49
  3. CGPA with all B: 190 / 49 = 3.88
  4. Quality points for all A: 18 x 5 = 90, so total = 118 + 90 = 208
  5. CGPA with all A: 208 / 49
AnswerAll B gives about 3.88. All A gives about 4.24

This shows how slowly a CGPA moves once you have many units behind you. Even a perfect semester lifted the CGPA from 3.81 to about 4.24, not to 5.0. That is why starting well matters, and why every semester counts.

How to Improve Your CGPA

  1. Work out the target. Use the projection method to see what grades you need over the remaining units.
  2. Protect high-unit courses. A 4-unit course affects your result more than a 1-unit course, so give it extra time.
  3. Fix F grades. An F adds units but zero points. Retaking and passing the course raises your total.
  4. Do not neglect small courses. A string of D and E grades in 1 or 2-unit courses adds up.
  5. Use continuous assessment. CA marks are the easiest to secure if you attend and submit on time.
  6. Practise past questions for each course before the exam.
⚠️
Do not trust unofficial calculators blindly

Always check the grading scheme and rules in your school handbook. Different institutions may treat repeated courses and grade ranges differently.

Common Mistakes

  • Averaging semester GPAs instead of using total quality points and total units.
  • Using the wrong grade scale, for example a 4.0 scale on a 5.0 school.
  • Forgetting to include failed courses in the total units.
  • Ignoring the effect of carry-overs when planning your final year.

What to Do Today

  • Find your school's grading scale in the handbook
  • List all courses so far with units and grades
  • Compute total quality points and total units
  • Divide to get your current CGPA
  • Project next semester with all B and all A to see the range
  • Choose two courses where you will focus your extra effort

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?

GPA is your average for one semester. CGPA is the cumulative average across all your semesters so far, weighted by credit units.

How do I calculate CGPA?

Multiply each course's grade point by its credit units to get quality points, add all the quality points, then divide by the total credit units across all semesters.

What grade points are used on the 5.0 scale?

A common scheme is A = 5, B = 4, C = 3, D = 2, E = 1 and F = 0. Some schools differ slightly, so confirm in your handbook.

Can I raise a low CGPA?

Yes, but the more units you have already completed, the more slowly it moves. Strong results in later semesters and resitting failed courses can help.

Is the CGPA average of my GPAs?

Not exactly. CGPA weights each semester by its units, so a semester with more units has a bigger effect than a lighter one.
