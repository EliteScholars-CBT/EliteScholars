NECO and WAEC Economics: the questions that keep repeating, answered

Demand and supply, elasticity, opportunity cost, money and national income keep showing up. Here are the repeating question types with worked calculations and clean theory answers.

Why Economics questions repeat

The Economics syllabus is built on a small set of ideas that appear in every term: scarcity, demand and supply, costs, money, and national income. Examiners reword the same ideas each year. If you know a core concept thoroughly, you can handle the question in whatever words it is presented. NECO and WAEC both examine the same broad areas, and the details of format can change, so check the current syllabus on the official portal.

Scarcity, choice and opportunity cost

This is the foundation. Human wants are unlimited but resources are limited, so we must choose. The next best alternative given up is the opportunity cost. A scale of preference is a list of wants in order of importance.

Typical question: "Explain opportunity cost with an example." Write: opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made. Example: a student has money for either a textbook or a pair of shoes. If she buys the textbook, the shoes are the opportunity cost. For a farmer who uses land for cassava instead of yam, the yam that could have been grown is the opportunity cost. Always include a short example, because it proves you understand the idea.

Demand, supply and equilibrium

The law of demand says that, other things equal, the higher the price, the lower the quantity demanded. The law of supply says that the higher the price, the greater the quantity supplied. Equilibrium is where the two meet.

Know the difference between a change in quantity demanded (caused by a price change, a movement along the curve) and a change in demand (caused by income, taste, price of substitutes, population, a shift of the curve).

QuestionThe demand and supply equations for a commodity are Qd = 100 - 2P and Qs = 20 + 2P. Find the equilibrium price and quantity.
  1. At equilibrium Qd = Qs so 100 - 2P = 20 + 2P
  2. Collect like terms: 100 - 20 = 2P + 2P
  3. So 80 = 4P and P = 20
  4. Substitute P = 20 into Qd: 100 - 2(20) = 60
  5. Check with Qs: 20 + 2(20) = 60 which agrees
AnswerEquilibrium price = 20 and equilibrium quantity = 60

If asked what happens when government fixes a price below equilibrium (a price ceiling), explain that quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied, producing a shortage, queues and possibly black markets. A price above equilibrium (a price floor, often used for farm produce) creates a surplus.

Elasticity: the calculation everyone meets

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how strongly buyers react to a price change.

Price elasticity of demandPED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in priceIgnore the minus sign when interpreting. Greater than 1 means elastic. Less than 1 means inelastic.
QuestionWhen the price of a good falls from 50 naira to 40 naira, the quantity demanded rises from 100 units to 130 units. Calculate the price elasticity of demand and say whether demand is elastic or inelastic.
  1. Percentage change in quantity = (130 - 100) / 100 x 100 = 30 percent
  2. Percentage change in price = (40 - 50) / 50 x 100 = -20 percent
  3. PED = 30 / -20 = -1.5
  4. Ignore the sign and compare with 1: 1.5 is greater than 1
AnswerPED = 1.5 so demand is elastic

Be ready to explain what determines elasticity: availability of substitutes, whether the good is a necessity or a luxury, the share of income spent on it, and time. Salt and drugs for chronic illness tend to be inelastic. A particular brand of soft drink with many substitutes tends to be elastic.

Revenue link: when demand is elastic, a price cut raises total revenue. When it is inelastic, a price cut lowers it. This is a favourite theory question.

Production and costs

Know the four factors of production: land, labour, capital and entrepreneur, and their rewards: rent, wages, interest and profit. Division of labour (specialisation) increases output but may cause boredom and dependence. Cost questions are usually short calculations.

QuestionA firm produces 50 units at a total cost of 500 naira. Total fixed cost is 200 naira. Find the average cost, the total variable cost and the average variable cost.
  1. Average cost = total cost / output = 500 / 50 = 10 naira
  2. Total variable cost = total cost - total fixed cost = 500 - 200 = 300 naira
  3. Average variable cost = 300 / 50 = 6 naira
AnswerAverage cost = 10 naira, TVC = 300 naira, AVC = 6 naira

Remember: average fixed cost falls as output rises because the fixed cost is spread over more units. Marginal cost is the extra cost of producing one more unit.

Money, banking and inflation

Common theory questions ask for the functions of money (medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account, standard of deferred payment), the qualities of good money (acceptability, durability, portability, divisibility, scarcity), and the functions of the central bank, such as issuing currency, acting as banker to the government and commercial banks, and controlling the money supply.

On inflation, define it as a persistent rise in the general price level. Give causes (too much money chasing too few goods, rising production costs, higher import prices) and effects (falling purchasing power, savers lose, borrowers may gain), and remedies (tighter monetary policy, increased production, controlling government spending). Write balanced answers with causes, effects and remedies.

National income and international trade

Know the three approaches to measuring national income: output, income and expenditure. Learn the difference between GDP and GNP: GNP equals GDP plus net income from abroad. For trade, know the reasons for trade (uneven distribution of resources), the meaning of balance of trade versus balance of payments, and the arguments for and against protection. ECOWAS and OPEC functions are common: explain their objectives clearly and mention Nigeria as a member.

⚠️
Do not mix up related terms

Quantity demanded versus demand, GDP versus GNP, balance of trade versus balance of payments. Mixing these up is one of the most common ways to drop marks, so write a one-line definition for each in your notes.

How to write theory answers

  • Define the term first, in one sentence.
  • Give the number of points required, each explained briefly.
  • Draw and label a diagram when the topic involves curves.
  • Use Nigerian examples such as cocoa, cassava, crude oil, the naira and local markets.
  • For calculations, write the formula, substitute and give units such as naira or percent.

What to do today

  • Solve one equilibrium problem and sketch the demand and supply diagram
  • Calculate PED for two new sets of numbers
  • Write out the definitions of demand, supply, elasticity and opportunity cost
  • List causes, effects and remedies of inflation
  • Practise one cost calculation with fixed and variable costs

Frequently asked questions

What topics repeat in NECO and WAEC Economics?

Demand and supply, elasticity, production and costs, money and banking, national income, population, public finance and international trade appear regularly. Check the current syllabus for the full list.

How do I calculate price elasticity of demand?

Divide the percentage change in quantity demanded by the percentage change in price. If the answer, ignoring the sign, is greater than 1, demand is elastic, and if it is less than 1, demand is inelastic.

How do I find equilibrium price and quantity?

Set quantity demanded equal to quantity supplied, solve for price, then substitute the price into either equation to find the quantity.

Should I draw diagrams in Economics essays?

Yes, when a question involves demand, supply, costs or equilibrium. Label both axes, name each curve and mark the equilibrium point. A neat diagram with a short explanation often earns extra marks.
