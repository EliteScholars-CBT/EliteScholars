NECO SSCE grading explained: what A1 to F9 really mean and how to aim for credits

A1, B3, C6, F9: your NECO result is a row of codes, and each code decides what you can do next. Here is what every grade means, which ones count as a credit, and how to plan your marks.

The nine grades at a glance

Your NECO SSCE result does not show a percentage in each subject. It shows a grade code from A1 down to F9. Each code stands for a band of marks, and each band carries a meaning: excellent, very good, credit, pass or fail.

The scale most Nigerian students know is below. These are the commonly published score ranges, so check the official NECO portal or your result slip to confirm them for your year.

The line that matters

The line between C6 and D7 is the one that decides your future. C6 is a credit. D7 is only a pass. One mark can move you across that line.

What "credit" really means

A credit is any grade from A1 to C6. When a school says "five credits", it means five subjects where you got A1, B2, B3, C4, C5 or C6. A D7 or E8 does not count toward that number, even though it is technically a pass.

This is why two students with the same number of "passes" can end up in very different places. One has six credits and applies for Law. The other has six D7s and is told to resit. The marks may look close, but the labels are not.

Which subjects need credits

For most university courses you need credits in English Language and Mathematics, plus credits in the subjects tied to your course. A Medicine applicant looks at Biology, Chemistry, Physics. A Law applicant looks at Literature-in-English, Government or CRS, and so on. For polytechnics and colleges of education, the minimum number of credits can be lower. Rules change, so read the current requirements from the institution itself and not from a friend or a forum.

The practical rule: English and Maths first. Nothing hurts more than three As and a D7 in Maths, because the one weak subject blocks the course.

A quick worked example

QuestionA student scores 52 in English, 47 in Mathematics, 61 in Biology, 58 in Economics and 38 in Civic Education. Give each grade and count the credits.
  1. 52 falls in 50 to 54 so English is C6
  2. 47 falls in 45 to 49 so Mathematics is D7
  3. 61 falls in 60 to 64 so Biology is C4
  4. 58 falls in 55 to 59 so Economics is C5
  5. 38 is below 40 so Civic Education is F9
  6. Credits are English, Biology and Economics
AnswerThree credits (C6, C4, C5), one pass (D7) and one fail (F9). The student has no Mathematics credit, so a resit of Mathematics is the first priority.

Notice what the example teaches. A raw score of 47 looks close to 50, yet it sits in a different class. Three more marks in Mathematics would have changed the entire plan.

How to plan your marks, not just hope

Instead of "I will read everything", work from targets. Decide what grade you need in each subject and work backwards.

  • Safety target: aim for 60 or above in your main subjects. That gives you a buffer of ten marks above the C6 line. Many students aim exactly for 50 and miss by two marks on exam day.
  • Weak subject rule: give your weakest subject the most hours, because every mark there moves a grade faster than the last mark in your best subject.
  • Objective first: in subjects with objective and essay parts, practise both. A student who answers every objective question but leaves essay questions blank loses a full grade.

Each step up the ladder is only five marks in the middle bands. That is why a focused fortnight on past questions can lift a D7 into a credit.

What if you already have a D7, E8 or F9?

A weak grade is not the end, but it needs a plan. First, find out whether the subject is one your target course actually requires. A D7 in Civic Education matters far less to a Medicine applicant than a D7 in Chemistry.

Second, if the subject is required, plan a resit. Look at the NECO registration notice for the current resit options and dates, and do not rely on hearsay. Third, change how you prepare. Most repeated low grades come from the same causes: reading notes without solving questions, skipping the topics that feel hard, and running out of time in the paper.

Use past questions as your main tool. Time yourself, mark yourself against the marking approach, and list every topic where you lost marks. Then study only those topics for three days and test again. That loop is how a D7 becomes a C5.

Mistakes that cost students grades

Common mistake

  • Reading all subjects equally
  • Ignoring the instructions on the paper
  • Leaving blanks in essay sections

Better way

  • Giving extra time to the weakest subject
  • Reading each instruction twice before answering
  • Writing something relevant for every question

Another mistake is never checking the real result. When your result is released, read the grade, not just the "passed" message. Confirm the subjects, grades and your personal details on the official portal, and report errors to NECO through the official channels.

What to do today

  • Write down the grade you want in each of your subjects
  • Circle English and Mathematics as the first priority
  • Find the admission requirements for your target course on the school's own website
  • Attempt one past question paper in your weakest subject this week
  • Mark it honestly and convert your score to a grade using the table

Frequently asked questions

What is a credit pass in NECO?

A credit pass is any grade from A1 to C6. D7 and E8 are passes but not credits, and F9 is a fail.

Is C6 a credit in NECO?

Yes. C6 is the lowest credit grade. Many institutions accept C6 when they ask for credits, though a few courses ask for higher grades in key subjects.

Is D7 good enough for university admission?

Usually not. D7 and E8 are passes, but most universities ask for credit-level grades (C6 or better) in the subjects that matter for the course. Check the requirements of your target school.

How many credits do I need in NECO?

Most universities ask for five credits including English Language and Mathematics, but requirements differ by course and school. Always confirm with the admission office or the school's brochure.

Does NECO use the same grading as WAEC?

Both use the same nine-point scale from A1 to F9. Always confirm the exact score ranges on your own result and the official NECO portal.
