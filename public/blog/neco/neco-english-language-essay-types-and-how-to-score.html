NECO English Language essays: formal letter, article, speech and narrative, with a model outline for each

Most candidates lose essay marks on format and expression, not on ideas. Here is how to set out a formal letter, article, speech and narrative, with an outline you can reuse.

What the examiner is actually marking

A NECO English essay is not graded on how clever your idea is. Examiners look at three things: content (did you answer this exact question), organisation (paragraphs, format, flow) and expression (grammar, spelling, vocabulary, punctuation). Two of those three are things you can fix with practice and a plan.

The most common reason candidates drop marks is that they write the wrong type. They are asked for a speech and write a story. They are asked for an article and write a letter to the editor. So your first ten seconds on any essay question should go to one task: find the type, the audience and the topic.

1Find the type
2Name the audience
3Plan 4 or 5 points
4Write
5Proofread for 3 minutes

Step zero: spend five minutes planning

Many candidates start writing immediately and run out of ideas halfway. Instead, jot down on your rough sheet: the type, who you are writing to, and four or five points in order. A five-minute plan makes the writing faster, and your paragraphs will not repeat each other.

A simple formula for almost every essay: introduction (2-3 sentences), three or four body paragraphs with one idea each, and a conclusion that closes the matter. Each body paragraph starts with a topic sentence, then explains or gives an example from Nigerian life that the examiner will recognise.

The formal letter

A formal letter goes to an official: a principal, a commissioner, an editor, a company manager. The layout is part of your score.

  • Your address at the top right (not your name, unless the question says so), then the date below it.
  • Receiver's title and address on the left.
  • Salutation: Dear Sir or Dear Madam.
  • Heading (subject line) in capitals or underlined, stating the purpose in one line.
  • Body: paragraph 1 states why you are writing, the next two or three give details and reasons, the last asks for action or thanks the reader.
  • Ending: Yours faithfully (when you used Dear Sir or Madam), then your signature and full name.

Common mistake

  • Writing Dear Sir and ending with Yours sincerely
  • Pouring out anger and insults at an official
  • Starting with a long story before the purpose

Better way

  • Dear Sir ends with Yours faithfully
  • Firm, polite and respectful tone
  • Purpose stated in the first sentence

Model outline for a letter to the principal about poor water supply in the school: paragraph 1, the problem and since when. Paragraph 2, how it affects students (hygiene, hostel, classes). Paragraph 3, your suggestions (borehole repairs, water tank, a duty roster). Paragraph 4, thanks and a request for a reply. An informal letter to a friend is different: it uses Dear plus the first name, a relaxed tone, and ends with Yours lovingly or Your friend. Do not mix the two.

The article

An article is written for readers of a newspaper, magazine or school bulletin. It is not a letter, so there is no Dear Sir and no Yours faithfully. The format is simple.

  1. A catchy title in capitals.
  2. Your by-line: By Chinedu Okeke (use a name, not your real one, if the question warns you).
  3. An opening that grabs attention: a question, a short scene, or a plain statement of the problem.
  4. Body paragraphs that discuss causes, effects and what should be done.
  5. A conclusion that leaves the reader with a final thought.

Example title: WHY THE YOUTH SHOULD LEAVE THEIR PHONES BEFORE BEDTIME. Notice how it already tells the reader what the article will argue. Address your readers directly: "we", "our schools", "our communities".

The speech

A speech is meant to be heard, so the language is spoken, direct and persuasive. Candidates often forget the opening and closing lines, which is where many marks are.

  • Title (for example: Speech delivered at the Inter-House Sports Debate).
  • Greetings: Mr Chairman, the principal, members of staff, my fellow students, ladies and gentlemen.
  • Introduction: state the topic and why it matters.
  • Body: three or four strong points with a short example each.
  • Conclusion: a call to action and "Thank you for listening."

Use rhetorical questions ("What will become of our future if we do nothing?"), the word "we" and short sentences. A speech that starts "Dear Sir" has lost the format marks immediately.

⚠️
Letter, article and speech are not the same

A letter needs an address and a Yours faithfully. An article needs a title and by-line. A speech needs greetings and Thank you. Check what you are asked for before you write line one.

The narrative essay

A narrative tells a story, usually based on a title such as "The day I will never forget" or "An experience that taught me honesty". Here the content is judged on how well the story is built.

  1. OpeningSet the scene and the characters in two or three sentences
  2. Rising actionShow what went wrong or what you wanted
  3. ClimaxThe turning point, in vivid detail
  4. EndingWhat happened next and the lesson learned

Use the past tense from beginning to end and keep it that way. Show, do not just tell: instead of "I was afraid", write "My hands shook and my throat went dry as the bus stopped." Describe a sound, a smell, a colour. Keep the plot simple. A story with one clear incident, well told, beats a story with six events squeezed in.

A descriptive essay (describing a market, a festival or a person) uses the same skills but follows a place or time order instead of a plot. An argumentative essay takes a side, gives three reasons, answers the opposing view in one paragraph and concludes firmly.

Worked example: planning a full answer

QuestionYou are asked: Write an article for your school magazine on the dangers of examination malpractice.
  1. Type is article so write a title and by-line
  2. Audience is fellow students and teachers so use we and our
  3. Opening: a short scene of a candidate caught with a note in the hall
  4. Body 1: consequences such as cancelled results and a record that follows you
  5. Body 2: causes such as laziness and fear of failure
  6. Body 3: solutions such as early revision and help from teachers
  7. Conclusion: an honest score is worth more than a stolen one
  8. Proofread tenses and spelling
AnswerTitle, by-line and four paragraphs with a clear introduction, three body ideas and a conclusion

Notice how every body paragraph has one job. That is why the planning pays off: the examiner can see your structure at a glance.

Expression: the marks you can control

  • Tenses: do not jump between past and present in a story.
  • Concord: "The students were late", not "The students was late".
  • Spelling: check the words you always get wrong: necessary, receive, separate, government, environment.
  • Punctuation: end each sentence properly and do not join sentences with a comma only.
  • Slang and text language: do not use "u", "dem" or "wetin" in an exam essay.

Learn five or six good linking words (however, moreover, in addition, as a result, on the other hand) and use them correctly. Overusing big words you are not sure about usually costs marks.

What to do today

  • Write one formal letter and check the layout against this post
  • Write one speech with greetings and a closing line
  • Plan an article in five minutes and write it in 35
  • Read your own work aloud and fix every tense error
  • Collect five words you misspell and learn them

Frequently asked questions

What types of essay come in NECO English Language?

Past papers have asked for formal and informal letters, articles for a newspaper or magazine, speeches, narrative or descriptive essays, and argumentative or debate-style writing. Read the question for the type before you plan anything.

How many words should a NECO English essay be?

Follow the word limit printed on your question paper or in the current syllabus. As a habit, write a complete essay with a clear introduction, body and conclusion instead of stopping early or rambling far beyond the limit.

Do I lose marks for using the wrong letter format?

Yes. Content, organisation and expression are all marked, and a letter without the right address layout, salutation and ending shows poor organisation. Format errors are among the easiest marks to protect.

How can I improve my English expression quickly?

Write short, correct sentences, vary your openings, and read your answer once for spelling, tense and punctuation. Simple English written correctly scores better than long words used wrongly.
