Demand and supply explained with Nigerian market examples (and how to draw the graphs)

Why does tomato get expensive in the dry season and cheap at harvest? Demand and supply answers it. Learn the laws, the five-step graph method and a solved equilibrium question.

Start with the market, not the definition

Go to any market in Lagos, Onitsha or Kano in the dry season and watch the price of tomatoes. Fewer baskets arrive, buyers still want them, and the price climbs. At harvest, trucks arrive full, sellers compete, and the same basket costs far less. You have just watched demand and supply at work. Economics only puts words and a graph on what traders already know.

Demand is the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at each price over a given period. "Able" matters: wanting a car you cannot pay for is not demand. Supply is the quantity producers are willing and able to offer for sale at each price over a given period.

The law of demand and the demand curve

The law of demand says: other things being equal (ceteris paribus), the higher the price, the lower the quantity demanded, and the lower the price, the higher the quantity demanded. Price and quantity move in opposite directions, so the curve slopes down from left to right.

Why do people behave this way? Three reasons you can quote in an exam:

  • Income effect: when the price falls, your money buys more, so you can afford more units.
  • Substitution effect: if garri gets cheaper while rice stays the same, some people switch from rice to garri.
  • Diminishing marginal utility: the second plate of rice gives less satisfaction than the first, so you only buy more if the price drops.

A demand schedule is simply a table of prices and quantities. Plot price on the vertical axis (Y) and quantity on the horizontal axis (X), join the points, and you have the demand curve.

⚠️
Exam trap

A demand curve never shows a rise in demand when the price of the good itself falls. That is a movement along the curve, called an extension of demand. Writing "demand increased" here costs marks.

Movement along the curve versus shift of the curve

This is the single most tested idea in the topic, so get it clear.

  • Movement (extension or contraction): caused only by a change in the good's own price.
  • Shift (increase or decrease in demand): caused by any other factor, called the determinants of demand.

The main determinants of demand are: consumers' income, tastes and fashion, prices of related goods (substitutes and complements), population size, expectations about future prices, and advertising.

Nigerian examples: if the price of petrol rises sharply, demand for cars that use less fuel may increase, and demand for generators may fall. If bread becomes dearer, demand for yam and garri may increase because they are substitutes. If the price of bread rises, demand for butter or margarine falls, because they are complements. During festive periods, demand for rice, chicken and fabric shifts right.

The law of supply and what shifts it

The law of supply says: other things being equal, the higher the price, the greater the quantity supplied. The curve slopes upward because a higher price gives producers more profit and encourages them to produce more, and because higher output often has higher unit costs that only a higher price can cover.

The determinants of supply are: cost of production (wages, fuel, raw materials), technology, taxes and subsidies, weather (especially for farm produce), number of sellers, and expectations. A poultry farmer whose feed price jumps from one month to the next supplies fewer eggs at every price, so the whole supply curve shifts left. A government subsidy on fertiliser lowers costs and shifts the supply of maize to the right.

Common confusion

  • Price of tomatoes rises so demand falls
  • A fall in supply makes price rise so demand extends

Correct idea

  • Price of tomatoes rises so quantity demanded falls
  • Supply shifts left, price rises, quantity demanded contracts along the curve

Equilibrium: where the curves meet

The market clears at the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. This is the equilibrium price, and the matching quantity is the equilibrium quantity. Above it there is a surplus (supply exceeds demand), so sellers cut prices. Below it there is a shortage, so buyers bid prices up. The market keeps pushing toward the middle.

QuestionGiven Qd = 120 - 2P and Qs = 20 + 3P (P in naira hundreds, Q in units), find equilibrium price and quantity. Then find what happens if demand rises so that Qd = 140 - 2P.
  1. Set Qd = Qs: 120 - 2P = 20 + 3P
  2. Collect terms: 100 = 5P so P = 20
  3. Substitute into Qd: 120 - 2(20) = 80 and check Qs: 20 + 3(20) = 80
  4. New demand: 140 - 2P = 20 + 3P so 120 = 5P and P = 24
  5. New quantity: 140 - 48 = 92 and check Qs: 20 + 72 = 92
AnswerFirst equilibrium: P = 20 and Q = 80. After demand rises: P = 24 and Q = 92. A rise in demand raises both price and quantity.

How to draw the graph in five steps

Examiners award marks for neat, labelled diagrams. Follow this order every time:

  1. Draw the axesPrice on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis, with an arrow on each
  2. Draw the first curvesDemand slopes down as D, supply slopes up as S, crossing at E
  3. Mark equilibriumDrop dotted lines from E to the axes and label P and Q
  4. Shift one curveDraw the new curve parallel to the old one and name it D1 or S1
  5. Read the resultMark the new equilibrium E1 with P1 and Q1 and state in one sentence what changed

Shift rules to memorise: demand right means higher price and higher quantity. Demand left means lower price and lower quantity. Supply right means lower price and higher quantity. Supply left means higher price and lower quantity. Only shift one curve at a time in an answer unless the question says otherwise.

When government fixes the price

Governments sometimes override the market. A price ceiling (maximum price) set below equilibrium is meant to protect consumers, for example a cap on the price of a staple. Using the numbers above, suppose the maximum price is 15 instead of 20. Then Qd = 120 - 30 = 90 but Qs = 20 + 45 = 65, leaving a shortage of 25 units. Shortages bring queues, rationing and black-market prices above the legal limit.

A price floor (minimum price) set above equilibrium is meant to protect producers, as with guaranteed prices for farm produce. It creates a surplus that someone, usually the government, must buy or store. In an essay, always give one benefit and one problem of each.

🔑
Remember

Demand and supply tell you the direction of change. If you can say which curve moved, which way, and what happened to price and quantity, you have answered most questions in this topic.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a change in demand and a change in quantity demanded?

A change in quantity demanded is caused only by the good's own price and shows as a movement along the same curve. A change in demand is caused by anything else, such as income or taste, and shifts the whole curve.

What is equilibrium price?

It is the price at which the quantity buyers want equals the quantity sellers offer. At that price there is no shortage and no surplus, so there is no pressure for the price to move.

Why does the demand curve slope downwards?

Because when price falls, buyers can afford more, switch from dearer substitutes and each extra unit gives less satisfaction. So they buy more at lower prices.

What happens when government fixes a price below equilibrium?

Quantity demanded becomes greater than quantity supplied, so a shortage appears. Queues, rationing and black markets usually follow.

Does a fall in price of a good shift its supply curve?

No. The good's own price only moves you along the supply curve. Supply shifts when costs, technology, taxes, weather or the number of sellers change.
