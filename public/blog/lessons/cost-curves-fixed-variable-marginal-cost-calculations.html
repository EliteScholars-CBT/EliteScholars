Fixed, Variable, Average and Marginal Cost: Calculations That Appear in Exams

Total cost, average cost and marginal cost questions are among the easiest marks in Economics if you know the formulas. Here is every formula, one full cost table and the traps examiners set.

The seven cost formulas you must know

Almost every cost question in WAEC, NECO and JAMB Economics comes from these relationships. Learn them as a family, not as separate items.

Cost familyTC = TFC + TVC ; AFC = TFC / Q ; AVC = TVC / Q ; ATC = TC / Q ; MC = change in TC / change in QTFC total fixed cost, TVC total variable cost, TC total cost, Q quantity of output
  • Fixed cost: does not change with output, such as factory rent or a generator that you must pay for whether or not you produce.
  • Variable cost: rises as output rises, such as flour in a bakery, packaging and casual labour.
  • Average cost (ATC): cost per unit. ATC = AFC + AVC.
  • Marginal cost: the extra cost of making one more unit.

QuestionFrom the table, find the marginal cost of the 5th loaf batch and the average total cost of producing 5 batches.
  1. TC at 4 = 100 + 120 = 220 and TC at 5 = 100 + 160 = 260
  2. MC of the 5th = 260 - 220 = 40
  3. ATC at 5 = TC / Q = 260 / 5 = 52
  4. Check: AFC 20 + AVC 32 = 52
AnswerMC = N40 and ATC = N52

What the numbers tell you

  • AFC keeps falling (100, 50, 33.33, 25, 20, 16.67) because the same N100 is shared among more units. This is why a big producer can spread overheads.
  • MC falls first, then rises (40, 30, 20, 30, 40, 60). Early on, extra workers specialise and output per worker rises. Later, the law of diminishing returns sets in and each extra unit costs more.
  • ATC is U-shaped: it falls to N52 at 5 units, then rises to 53.33 at 6.

The marginal-average rule

Notice that at 6 units, MC (N60) is above ATC (N53.33), so ATC is being pulled upward. At 4 and 5 units, MC (30 and 40) is below ATC, so ATC keeps falling. MC crosses ATC at its lowest point. The same applies to AVC. Your exam diagram should show the MC curve cutting both the AVC and ATC curves from below at their minimum points.

💡
Exam tip

If you are asked where MC meets ATC, say at the minimum point of ATC. Never say at the maximum.

Also remember that the gap between ATC and AVC is AFC. It shrinks as output grows, so the ATC and AVC curves get closer together but never touch.

Traps examiners set

  • Fixed cost at zero output is not zero. TC at Q = 0 equals TFC.
  • MC uses change in TC, not TC itself. Dividing TC by Q gives average cost, not marginal cost.
  • Dividing by the wrong Q. For MC between Q = 4 and Q = 6, divide by 2.
  • Calling wages fixed. A salaried manager is fixed. Pay-per-day casual labour is variable.
  • Missing units. Write N and the output unit in every answer.
QuestionA firm has TFC of N500. At 20 units TVC is N900, and at 25 units TVC is N1,200. Find ATC at 25 units and MC between 20 and 25 units.
  1. TC at 20 = 500 + 900 = 1,400
  2. TC at 25 = 500 + 1,200 = 1,700
  3. ATC at 25 = 1,700 / 25 = 68
  4. MC = (1,700 - 1,400) / (25 - 20) = 300 / 5 = 60
AnswerATC = N68 and MC = N60 per unit

What to do today

  • Write the seven formulas from memory
  • Rebuild the bakery table without looking
  • Sketch the MC, AVC, ATC and AFC curves on one diagram
  • Do one question where only TVC and TFC are given
  • Explain in one sentence why MC cuts ATC at the minimum

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between fixed cost and variable cost?

Fixed cost does not change with the level of output, for example rent. Variable cost changes as output changes, for example raw materials and hourly wages.

How do you calculate marginal cost?

Marginal cost is the change in total cost divided by the change in output. For one extra unit, it is the new total cost minus the old total cost.

Why does the marginal cost curve cut the average cost curve at its lowest point?

When marginal cost is below average cost, it pulls the average down. When it is above, it pulls the average up. So the two meet where the average stops falling and starts rising.

Does average fixed cost ever rise?

No. Fixed cost is spread over more units as output rises, so average fixed cost always falls.
