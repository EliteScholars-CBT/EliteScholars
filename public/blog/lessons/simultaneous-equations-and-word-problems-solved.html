Simultaneous Equations and Word Problems: How to Set Them Up and Solve Them

Learn to solve simultaneous equations by elimination and substitution, then turn word problems into equations step by step. All examples are checked.

What simultaneous equations mean

When two unknowns, say x and y, appear in two equations, you need both equations to find them. Each equation alone has infinitely many solutions. Together, they usually give exactly one pair. Graphically, the answer is the point where the two lines cross.

Method 1: substitution

Use this when one equation lets you write one letter in terms of the other.

QuestionSolve x + y = 10 and 3x + 2y = 24.
  1. From the first, y = 10 - x
  2. Substitute into the second: 3x + 2(10 - x) = 24
  3. 3x + 20 - 2x = 24 so x = 4
  4. Then y = 10 - 4 = 6
  5. Check: 3(4) + 2(6) = 12 + 12 = 24
Answerx = 4 and y = 6

Method 2: elimination

Add or subtract the equations so that one letter cancels.

QuestionSolve 2x + 3y = 12 and 5x - 3y = 9.
  1. The y terms are +3y and -3y, so add the equations
  2. 7x = 21 so x = 3
  3. Put x = 3 in the first: 6 + 3y = 12 so 3y = 6 and y = 2
  4. Check in the second: 15 - 6 = 9
Answerx = 3 and y = 2

If no letter matches, multiply one or both equations first. For 3x + 2y = 16 and 2x + 5y = 18, multiply the first by 5 and the second by 2 to match the y terms: 15x + 10y = 80 and 4x + 10y = 36. Subtract: 11x = 44, so x = 4. Put x = 4 in the first: 12 + 2y = 16, so y = 2.

💡
Sign rule

Same signs on the letter you want to remove: subtract. Opposite signs: add.

Turning word problems into equations

The skill that earns the marks is setting up. Use this routine:

  1. Name the unknownsWrite what x and y stand for, with units
  2. Find two factsEach fact in the question becomes one equation
  3. Write the equationsTranslate word by word
  4. SolveUse elimination or substitution
  5. Answer in wordsState the answer with units and check against the story
QuestionThree pens and two books cost N1,100. Two pens and one book cost N650. Find the price of a pen and of a book.
  1. Let a pen cost N p and a book cost N b
  2. Equation 1: 3p + 2b = 1100
  3. Equation 2: 2p + b = 650 so b = 650 - 2p
  4. Substitute: 3p + 2(650 - 2p) = 1100 which gives 3p + 1300 - 4p = 1100
  5. So -p = -200 and p = 200
  6. Then b = 650 - 400 = 250
  7. Check: 3(200) + 2(250) = 600 + 500 = 1100
AnswerA pen costs N200 and a book costs N250

Another type is the age or number problem. Two numbers add up to 45 and one is 4 times the other. Let the numbers be x and y: x + y = 45 and x = 4y. Substitute: 4y + y = 45, so y = 9 and x = 36. Check: 36 + 9 = 45 and 36 = 4 x 9. Correct.

Linear and quadratic pairs

JAMB and WAEC often pair a straight line with a curve. The method is always substitution.

QuestionSolve y = x + 1 and x2 + y2 = 25.
  1. Substitute y: x2 + (x + 1)2 = 25
  2. Expand: 2x2 + 2x + 1 = 25 so 2x2 + 2x - 24 = 0
  3. Divide by 2: x2 + x - 12 = 0 which factorises as (x + 4)(x - 3) = 0
  4. x = 3 gives y = 4 and x = -4 gives y = -3
AnswerThe solutions are (3, 4) and (-4, -3)

Note that a quadratic pair often has two pairs of answers. Never give only the x values. Find the y that goes with each x using the simpler equation.

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Subtracting a whole equation but forgetting to change every sign.
  • Finding x and forgetting y.
  • Not defining your letters, so the marker cannot follow.
  • Skipping the check against the original words of the problem.
  • Pairing the wrong x and y in a quadratic pair.

What to do today

  • Solve three pairs by elimination and three by substitution
  • Write the five-step routine on a card
  • Set up and solve two money word problems
  • Solve one linear and quadratic pair
  • Check every answer in both equations

Frequently asked questions

What are simultaneous equations?

They are two or more equations with the same unknowns that must be true at the same time. The solution is the set of values that satisfies all of them.

Should I use elimination or substitution?

Use elimination when the coefficients of one letter match or are easy to match. Use substitution when one equation already has a letter alone, such as y = x + 1.

How do I solve a linear and a quadratic equation together?

Make one letter the subject of the linear equation, substitute it into the quadratic, solve, and then find the matching values of the other letter.

How can I check my answer?

Put your values back into both original equations. They must both be true.
