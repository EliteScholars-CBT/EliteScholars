Probability Made Simple: Dice, Coins, Cards and Bags, With Solved Examples

Probability questions follow a small number of patterns. Learn the basic formula, the AND and OR rules, and how to handle bags with and without replacement.

The basic idea

Probability measures how likely an event is. It is a number from 0 to 1. A probability of 0 means impossible, 1 means certain, and 1/2 means even chance.

ProbabilityP(event) = favourable outcomes / total outcomesAll outcomes must be equally likely

Before anything else, list the sample space, which is every possible outcome. For a fair die it is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. For one coin it is head or tail. Two useful facts: the probabilities of all outcomes add up to 1, and P(not A) = 1 - P(A).

Dice and coins

QuestionTwo fair dice are thrown. Find the probability that the sum is 7, and that the sum is 10 or more.
  1. Total outcomes = 6 x 6 = 36
  2. Sum of 7: (1,6) (2,5) (3,4) (4,3) (5,2) (6,1) which is 6 outcomes so 6/36 = 1/6
  3. Sum of 10 or more: (4,6) (5,5) (6,4) (5,6) (6,5) (6,6) which is 6 outcomes so 6/36 = 1/6
AnswerBoth probabilities equal 1/6

For three fair coins there are 2 x 2 x 2 = 8 outcomes. Exactly two heads can happen as HHT, HTH or THH, so the probability is 3/8. At least one head is easier by the complement: P(no head) = 1/8, so P(at least one head) = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8.

💡
Shortcut

When you see the phrase at least one, work out the chance of none and subtract it from 1.

The AND and OR rules

  • OR (mutually exclusive events): add. P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).
  • AND (independent events): multiply. P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B).
  • OR when events can overlap: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B).

Take a standard pack of 52 playing cards. Find the probability of drawing a king or a heart. There are 4 kings and 13 hearts, but the king of hearts is counted in both, so P = 4/52 + 13/52 - 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13.

Common error

  • Adding probabilities for AND
  • Forgetting the overlap in cards

Correct approach

  • Multiply for AND
  • Subtract the overlap

Bags: with and without replacement

A bag has 5 red, 3 blue and 2 green balls, so 10 in total. Two are drawn one after the other.

QuestionWithout replacement, find the probability that both balls are red, and that one is red and one is blue.
  1. P(first red) = 5/10
  2. P(second red
Answerfirst red) = 4/9 because one red is gone and only 9 balls remain ; Both red = 5/10 x 4/9 = 20/90 = 2/9 ; Red then blue = 5/10 x 3/9 = 15/90 ; Blue then red = 3/10 x 5/9 = 15/90 ; Either order = 30/90 = 1/3

With replacement, the ball is put back, so the total stays at 10. P(both red) = 5/10 x 5/10 = 1/4. Always read the question for the words with or without replacement. If it says nothing but the items are drawn together, treat it as without replacement.

Tree diagrams

A tree diagram keeps your branches tidy. Write the probabilities on the branches, multiply along a path to get the chance of that path, and add the paths that satisfy the question. For the bag above, the first column has red 5/10, blue 3/10 and green 2/10. After a red, the second column has red 4/9, blue 3/9 and green 2/9. Each set of branches from one point must add up to 1, which is a useful way to catch mistakes.

Expected number of outcomes

If you toss a fair die 60 times, the expected number of sixes is 60 x 1/6 = 10. Probability does not promise exactly 10, only that it is the average you would expect over many trials.

What to do today

  • Write the sample space for two dice from memory
  • Solve three at least one questions using the complement
  • Draw a tree diagram for a bag without replacement
  • Practise one king or heart style overlap question
  • Check every answer lies between 0 and 1

Frequently asked questions

What is the basic formula for probability?

Probability = number of favourable outcomes divided by total number of possible outcomes, assuming all outcomes are equally likely.

When do I add and when do I multiply probabilities?

Add for OR (either event, when they cannot happen together). Multiply for AND (both events happen, one after the other).

What is the difference between with and without replacement?

With replacement, the item goes back and the total stays the same. Without replacement, the item is not returned, so the total falls by one for the next draw.

Can a probability be greater than 1?

No. Probabilities range from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).
