Sets and Venn Diagrams: How to Solve the Word Problems

Set questions look confusing until you learn the order for filling in a Venn diagram. Work through two-set and three-set problems with the right formulas.

Set language you need

A set is a collection of well-defined items. Curly brackets list the members, for example A = {2, 4, 6, 8}. These symbols appear in every exam:

  • Union (A U B): everything in A or B or both.
  • Intersection (A n B): only what is in both A and B.
  • Complement (A'): everything in the universal set that is not in A.
  • n(A): the number of members of A.
  • Universal set (U or the symbol for epsilon): the whole group under discussion.

In word problems, the words both and and mean intersection. The words either, or, at least one mean union. The word only means in that set and nothing else. Neither means outside all circles.

Union of two setsn(A U B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A n B)Subtract the overlap because it was counted twice

Two-set problems

QuestionIn a school of 60 students, 35 offer Economics, 30 offer Government and 12 offer both. Find how many offer Economics only, Government only and neither.
  1. Start with the overlap: both = 12
  2. Economics only = 35 - 12 = 23
  3. Government only = 30 - 12 = 18
  4. Union = 23 + 12 + 18 = 53
  5. Neither = 60 - 53 = 7
AnswerEconomics only = 23, Government only = 18, neither = 7

You can confirm with the formula: 35 + 30 - 12 = 53, and 60 - 53 = 7. Always find the overlap first. Many students make the mistake of writing 35 inside the Economics circle, which wrongly includes the students who also take Government.

🔑
Golden rule

The number you are given for a set is its TOTAL, including the overlap. The number you write in a region is only that region.

The order for filling a Venn diagram

  1. Draw itRectangle for the universal set and overlapping circles
  2. Middle firstFill the region common to all sets
  3. OverlapsSubtract the middle to get each two-set overlap
  4. Only regionsSubtract overlaps to get what is left in each circle
  5. OutsideTotal minus everything inside the circles

Three-set problems

These look scary but follow the same order. The key formula for three sets is n(A U B U C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - n(A n B) - n(B n C) - n(A n C) + n(A n B n C).

QuestionEvery student in a group of Biology (B), Chemistry (C) and Physics (P) offers at least one of them. n(B) = 25, n(C) = 22, n(P) = 18, n(B n C) = 9, n(B n P) = 7, n(C n P) = 6 and all three = 3. Find the total number of students and how many offer Biology only.
  1. Total = 25 + 22 + 18 - 9 - 7 - 6 + 3 = 46
  2. B and C only = 9 - 3 = 6
  3. B and P only = 7 - 3 = 4
  4. Biology only = 25 - 6 - 4 - 3 = 12
AnswerThere are 46 students and 12 offer Biology only

To check the diagram, find the other regions too. Chemistry only = 22 - 6 - 3 - 3 = 10 (taking away B and C only = 6, C and P only = 3 and all three = 3). Physics only = 18 - 4 - 3 - 3 = 8. Add up all seven regions: 12 + 10 + 8 + 6 + 4 + 3 + 3 = 46. It matches.

Reading set statements

Examiners also like a question that asks for the number of students who offer exactly two subjects. Add only the overlap-only regions: 6 + 4 + 3 = 13. Or at least two subjects, meaning the overlaps plus the middle: 13 + 3 = 16. The word exactly excludes the middle. The words at least include it.

For listing questions, if A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} and B = {2, 4, 6, 8}, then A n B = {2, 4}, A U B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8}, and n(A U B) = 7. Do not repeat a member twice in a union.

Common mistakes

  • Writing the given total in a region instead of subtracting the overlaps.
  • Forgetting the outside region (neither).
  • Double counting members when writing a union.
  • Mixing up exactly and at least.
  • Not checking that all regions add up to the total.

What to do today

  • Memorise the symbols for union and intersection and complement
  • Draw and fill a two-set diagram from the school example
  • Rebuild the three-set Venn without looking
  • Practise exactly two versus at least two
  • Check that your regions sum to the total

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for the union of two sets?

n(A union B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A intersection B).

Where do I start when filling a Venn diagram?

Start with the region that is in all the sets (the middle), then fill the overlaps, then the 'only' regions, and finally the outside.

What does the complement of a set mean?

A' is everything in the universal set that is not in A.

What does neither mean in a set word problem?

It refers to elements outside all the circles but still inside the universal set.
