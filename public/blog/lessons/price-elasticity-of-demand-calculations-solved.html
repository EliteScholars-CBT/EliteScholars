Price elasticity of demand: formula, types and calculations solved

Elasticity measures how strongly buyers react to a price change. Learn the formula, the five types, the total revenue test and four solved calculations, including income and cross elasticity.

What elasticity actually measures

If the price of a bag of rice rises, you will buy less. The law of demand tells you the direction. Elasticity tells you how much. Will buyers barely notice, or will they run away? Sellers need to know this: raising prices on a product people must buy brings more money, while raising prices on a product people can easily replace can lose money.

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of the good.

Price elasticity of demandPED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price% change = (new - old) / old x 100

Worked calculation 1: finding PED

Work in three small steps and never skip the percentage stage; that is where marks are earned.

QuestionThe price of a product rises from N100 to N120 and quantity demanded falls from 500 to 400 units. Calculate the price elasticity of demand and say what type it is.
  1. Percentage change in price = (120 - 100) / 100 x 100 = 20%
  2. Percentage change in quantity = (400 - 500) / 500 x 100 = -20%
  3. PED = -20 / 20 = -1
  4. Ignore the sign and take the size: 1
AnswerPED = 1 (taking the absolute value), so demand is unitary elastic.

The five types of price elasticity

Look at the size of the number (ignore the minus sign):

Value of PEDNameMeaning
Greater than 1ElasticQuantity changes by a bigger percentage than price
Less than 1InelasticQuantity changes by a smaller percentage than price
Equal to 1UnitaryQuantity and price change by the same percentage
Equal to 0Perfectly inelasticQuantity does not change at all (vertical curve)
InfinityPerfectly elasticBuyers will buy any amount at one price and nothing above it (horizontal curve)

Perfect elasticity and inelasticity are theoretical extremes. A life-saving drug with no substitute comes close to perfectly inelastic. A single seller of identical produce in a market full of identical sellers faces something close to perfectly elastic demand.

What makes demand elastic or inelastic

  • Availability of substitutes: the more close substitutes, the more elastic. Different brands of noodles are good substitutes, so demand for one brand is elastic.
  • Necessity or luxury: salt, kerosene and basic medicine are necessities with inelastic demand. Foreign holidays and designer clothes are luxuries with elastic demand.
  • Share of income: a rise in the price of matches hardly matters, so demand is inelastic. A rise in school fees or rent matters a lot.
  • Time period: in the short run people cannot change habits easily; over months they find alternatives, so demand becomes more elastic.
  • Habit and addiction: habitual goods tend to have inelastic demand.
  • Number of uses: the more uses a good has, the more elastic its demand tends to be.
💡
Quick test

Ask two questions: can I easily replace it, and can I wait without it? If yes to both, demand is probably elastic. If no, it is probably inelastic.

Why firms care: the total revenue test

Total revenue (TR) = price x quantity. What happens to it after a price change depends on elasticity.

Demand isPrice risesPrice falls
ElasticTR fallsTR rises
InelasticTR risesTR falls
UnitaryTR unchangedTR unchanged
QuestionA seller raises the price from N50 to N60 and quantity demanded drops from 200 to 170 units. Find PED, say whether demand is elastic or inelastic, and compare total revenue before and after.
  1. Price change = (60 - 50) / 50 x 100 = 20%
  2. Quantity change = (170 - 200) / 200 x 100 = -15%
  3. PED = 15 / 20 = 0.75 (absolute value)
  4. 0.75 is less than 1 so demand is inelastic
  5. TR before = 50 x 200 = N10,000 and TR after = 60 x 170 = N10,200
AnswerPED = 0.75, inelastic. Total revenue rises from N10,000 to N10,200, as expected when a seller raises price on inelastic demand.

This explains real behaviour. Governments tax goods with inelastic demand, such as fuel and cigarettes, because tax revenue stays high even when the price rises. Shops hold sales on elastic goods like clothes because a price cut brings enough extra buyers to raise revenue.

Income elasticity and cross elasticity

WAEC and NECO also ask about two relatives of PED.

Income elasticity of demandYED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in incomePositive for normal goods, negative for inferior goods
QuestionWhen a family's income rises from N80,000 to N100,000 a month, its monthly purchase of fish rises from 20 to 26 pieces. Calculate YED.
  1. Income change = (100,000 - 80,000) / 80,000 x 100 = 25%
  2. Quantity change = (26 - 20) / 20 x 100 = 30%
  3. YED = 30 / 25 = 1.2
AnswerYED = 1.2. It is positive so fish is a normal good, and above 1 so it is a luxury-type good for this family.

If YED is positive the good is normal; above 1 it is income elastic, below 1 income inelastic. If YED is negative the good is inferior.

Cross elasticity of demand (XED) = % change in quantity demanded of good A divided by % change in price of good B. If the price of good B rises by 10% and the quantity of good A demanded rises by 4%, XED = 4 / 10 = 0.4. It is positive, so A and B are substitutes. A negative XED means complements, such as bread and butter; a value near zero means unrelated goods.

Common mistakes in elasticity questions

  • Dividing by the new value instead of the old value when calculating percentage change.
  • Forgetting to multiply by 100, or inverting the formula and dividing price change by quantity change.
  • Saying "elasticity is 0.75 so demand is elastic". Below 1 means inelastic.
  • Confusing a flat, horizontal curve (perfectly elastic) with a vertical one (perfectly inelastic).
  • Not writing the final conclusion in words. Always end with the type of elasticity.

What to do today

  • Write the PED formula and the percentage change formula from memory
  • Redo the three solved sums above without looking
  • Make up one elastic and one inelastic example from your own neighbourhood
  • Learn the total revenue table by heart
  • Solve one YED question and one XED question and state the type of good

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for price elasticity of demand?

PED = percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in price. Percentage change is (new minus old) divided by old, multiplied by 100.

Why is price elasticity of demand usually negative?

Because price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions. Most textbooks and exams ignore the sign and give the absolute value, so state clearly which way you are working.

What does an elasticity of 1 mean?

It is unitary elasticity: quantity demanded changes by exactly the same percentage as price, so total revenue stays the same when price changes.

Why is demand for salt inelastic?

It is a necessity, it has no close substitute and it takes a tiny part of a household budget. A price rise barely changes how much people buy.

What does a negative income elasticity mean?

The good is inferior. As income rises, people buy less of it, for example cheap, low-quality substitutes they drop once they can afford better.
