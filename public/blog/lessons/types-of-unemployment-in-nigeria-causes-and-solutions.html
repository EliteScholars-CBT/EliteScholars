Types of unemployment, their causes and solutions: an Economics essay guide using Nigeria

Frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal and more: learn each type of unemployment with a Nigerian example, the causes, the solutions, and how to structure a full-marks essay answer.

Who counts as unemployed?

Many students lose marks on this topic by missing the definition. A person is unemployed when they are able and willing to work at the going wage, and are actively looking for a job, but cannot find one. Someone who chooses not to work, a full-time student, a retiree or a person too ill to work is not unemployed. These people are outside the labour force.

Unemployment rateUnemployment rate = Number unemployed / Labour force x 100Labour force = employed + unemployed
QuestionA country has 28 million employed people and 7 million unemployed people looking for work. Calculate the unemployment rate.
  1. Labour force = employed + unemployed = 28 + 7 = 35 million
  2. Rate = 7 / 35 x 100 = 20%
AnswerThe unemployment rate is 20%. These figures are made up for practice; for real data, check the National Bureau of Statistics.

Types of unemployment with Nigerian examples

Examiners usually ask you to "explain any four types". Give a definition and a Nigerian example for each.

  • Frictional unemployment: short-term joblessness while people move between jobs or search. A graduate who finishes NYSC and spends a few months interviewing is frictionally unemployed. Even a healthy economy has some of it.
  • Structural unemployment: a mismatch between the skills workers have and the skills employers need, or between where jobs are and where workers live. A tailor made redundant by factory-made clothing who lacks digital skills is an example, as is an arts graduate looking for roles that need coding.
  • Cyclical (demand-deficient) unemployment: caused by a fall in total demand during a recession. Firms sell less, so they cut staff. When the economy recovers, jobs return.
  • Seasonal unemployment: work that exists only at certain times of the year. Farm labourers between planting and harvest, or hotel staff outside the festive season.
  • Technological unemployment: machines replace labour. A bank that installs ATMs and mobile banking needs fewer counter staff.
  • Disguised unemployment (underemployment): more people work than needed, so some add nothing to output. Ten family members on a small plot that two could farm is a classic case.
  • Voluntary unemployment: people refuse available jobs at the going wage, perhaps expecting a better offer.

Memory hook

Frictional is between jobs, structural is skills mismatch, cyclical is recession, seasonal is time of year, technological is machines, disguised is too many on one job.

Causes of unemployment in Nigeria

When asked for causes, group them so your essay has shape.

  • Rapid population growth: the number of young people seeking jobs can grow faster than the jobs created.
  • Mismatch between education and the labour market: many courses are theoretical, with little practical or digital training.
  • Poor infrastructure: unreliable electricity and bad roads raise business costs, so firms hire fewer people or close.
  • Weak manufacturing and industry: an economy that depends heavily on a few sectors creates few jobs.
  • Rural-urban drift: young people leave farming for cities where jobs are scarce, leaving farms short of labour and cities crowded.
  • Low investment and a difficult business climate: high costs and insecurity discourage new firms.
  • Lack of capital and skills for self-employment: many want to start a business but cannot access funding or training.
  • Use of capital-intensive technology: machines replace workers.

Effects of unemployment

A strong answer covers the individual, the economy and society:

  • Individual: loss of income, falling skills, stress and dependence on relatives.
  • Economy: wasted labour means lower output, lower tax revenue and weaker demand for goods.
  • Society: more crime, social unrest, emigration of skilled people and pressure on family support.

Underemployment and the informal economy

Official unemployment figures can understate the problem. Someone who sells sachet water for two hours a day because nothing else is available is counted as employed, yet is clearly underemployed. A graduate driving a commercial motorbike is working, but far below their skill level. This is why economists look beyond the headline rate to hours worked, income earned and whether the job matches the person's training.

The informal sector, made up of traders, artisans, okada and tricycle riders and small food sellers, absorbs a very large share of Nigerian workers. It is a safety valve that keeps open unemployment lower than it might be, but jobs there are often unstable, low-paid and without pension or health cover. In an essay, you can score extra marks by noting that raising productivity in the informal sector, through small loans, skills training and simple business registration, is a practical way to turn survival work into decent work.

Youth unemployment deserves its own sentence in any Nigerian answer: young people enter the labour force in large numbers each year, so even a good growth rate must create many new jobs just to stand still. That is why skills and entrepreneurship feature so heavily in the solutions.

Solutions: match the remedy to the type

This is where good essays stand out. Do not just list solutions; pair them with the problem they fix.

TypeSuitable remedy
FrictionalBetter job information: job centres, online job boards, career fairs
StructuralRetraining, vocational and technical education, updated school curricula
CyclicalGovernment spending, tax cuts and credit to raise demand
SeasonalIrrigation, off-season farming, agro-processing and rural industries
TechnologicalReskilling workers and promoting labour-intensive projects where suitable
DisguisedMoving surplus labour into new rural industries or training for other work

General measures include improving power supply and roads so businesses can grow, supporting small and medium enterprises with loans and training, encouraging entrepreneurship and skills such as tailoring, ICT and agriculture in schools, family planning education to slow labour force growth in the long run, and public works programmes that employ people directly.

Weak essay line

  • Government should create jobs
  • Unemployment is bad

Strong essay line

  • Government should fund technical and vocational training to tackle structural unemployment
  • Unemployment cuts output and tax revenue, which limits government spending

How to write the essay

  1. DefineState clearly what unemployment means and who is outside the labour force
  2. ClassifyName your types with one Nigerian example each
  3. Explain causesGroup them into economic, educational and social
  4. Give solutionsPair each remedy with the type or cause it fixes
  5. ConcludeAdd one sentence on which measure matters most and why

Keep paragraphs short, use headings if the question allows, and always give a Nigerian example. Examiners reward application, not only definitions.

Frequently asked questions

What are the types of unemployment?

The main types are frictional, structural, cyclical (demand-deficient), seasonal, technological, disguised (underemployment) and voluntary unemployment.

What is the difference between frictional and structural unemployment?

Frictional unemployment is short-term, as people move between jobs or search for the right one. Structural unemployment lasts longer because workers' skills no longer match the jobs available.

What is disguised unemployment?

It occurs when more workers are employed than a job needs, so some add nothing to output. It is common on small family farms where removing a few workers would not reduce production.

How do you calculate the unemployment rate?

Unemployment rate = unemployed people divided by the labour force, multiplied by 100. The labour force means employed plus unemployed people who are willing and able to work.

Is a student without a job unemployed?

No. Full-time students, retired people and those not looking for work are outside the labour force, so they are not counted as unemployed.
