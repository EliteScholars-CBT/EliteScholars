National income: GDP, GNP and NNP explained with worked calculations

GDP, GNP and NNP are close cousins that exam questions love to mix up. See how they connect, the three methods of measuring national income, and a full calculation you can copy.

What national income means

National income is the total value of goods and services a country produces, and the income earned from producing them, in a given period, usually one year. Economists track it to see whether an economy is growing, to compare countries and to plan government spending. In an exam, you will be asked to define the terms, calculate one from another, and discuss the problems of measuring it.

The key to the topic is that GDP, GNP and NNP are one chain. Learn the chain and every calculation becomes simple addition and subtraction.

GDP, GNP and NNP: the chain

  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP): the value of all final goods and services produced within the country's borders in a year, whether by Nigerians or foreigners. A Chinese company running a factory in Ogun State adds to Nigeria's GDP.
  • Gross National Product (GNP): the value of output produced by a country's citizens and firms wherever in the world they are. Profit sent home by a Nigerian bank's branch in Ghana counts toward GNP.
  • Net National Product (NNP): GNP less depreciation, which is the wear and tear on machines and buildings used up in producing the output.
The chainGNP = GDP + NFIA, then NNP = GNP - DepreciationNFIA is net factor income from abroad: income Nigerians earn abroad minus income foreigners earn in Nigeria

NFIA can be negative. A country where foreign companies take out more profit than its citizens earn abroad will have GNP smaller than GDP. Only final goods are counted. Counting flour and then counting the bread made from it would count the flour twice, which is called double counting.

🔑
Easy memory

Domestic means location, inside the borders. National means ownership, who the producers are. Gross means before depreciation, Net means after.

The three methods of measuring national income

Every naira produced becomes someone's income and is then spent by someone. That is why there are three ways to measure the same total.

  • Output (product) method: add the value added at each stage of production in every sector (agriculture, industry, services). Using value added avoids double counting.
  • Income method: add all incomes earned from production: wages and salaries, rent, interest and profit.
  • Expenditure method: add all spending on final goods and services: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M).

In the expenditure formula, C is household consumption, I is investment by firms, G is government spending, X is exports and M is imports. Imports are subtracted because that spending went to foreign producers, not local ones.

Worked calculation: expenditure method

QuestionAn economy records these figures in N billion: consumption 600, investment 150, government spending 200, exports 120, imports 170. Net factor income from abroad is -30 and depreciation is 50. Calculate GDP, GNP and NNP.
  1. GDP = C + I + G + (X - M) = 600 + 150 + 200 + (120 - 170)
  2. GDP = 950 - 50 = 900
  3. GNP = GDP + NFIA = 900 + (-30) = 870
  4. NNP = GNP - depreciation = 870 - 50 = 820
AnswerGDP = N900 billion, GNP = N870 billion, NNP = N820 billion.

Worked calculation: income method and factor cost

Take the same economy. NNP at market prices is 820, but market prices include indirect taxes (like VAT) and exclude subsidies. To get the income that actually reaches producers, subtract net indirect taxes. That gives national income at factor cost.

QuestionUsing NNP at market prices of N820 billion, and indirect taxes less subsidies of N40 billion, find national income at factor cost. Check it against the income method where wages are 400, rent 80, interest 70 and profit 230.
  1. National income at factor cost = NNP at market price - (indirect taxes - subsidies)
  2. = 820 - 40 = 780
  3. Income method total = 400 + 80 + 70 + 230 = 780
  4. The two figures agree
AnswerNational income at factor cost = N780 billion, and the income method confirms it.

The chain also works backwards: 780 + 40 = 820 (NNP at market price), 820 + 50 = 870 (GNP), and 870 - (-30) = 900 (GDP). If your answer does not reconcile like this, an arithmetic slip is hiding somewhere.

Nominal versus real GDP and per capita income

If prices double and output stays the same, nominal GDP doubles even though nobody is better off. Real GDP removes the effect of price changes by using a price index called the GDP deflator.

Real GDPReal GDP = Nominal GDP / Price index x 100Use a base-year index of 100

With nominal GDP of N900 billion and a price index of 120, real GDP = 900 / 120 x 100 = N750 billion. Reading only the nominal figure would overstate growth.

Per capita income = national income divided by population. If GNP is N870 billion and the population is 30 million, per capita income = 870 billion / 30 million = N29,000 per person. It is a useful average but hides how unequal income can be. These numbers are invented to practise the method; for real figures, check the National Bureau of Statistics.

Problems of measuring national income in Nigeria

Examiners often ask for these. Learn them with a Nigerian illustration each:

  • Informal sector: roadside traders, artisans and many small farmers are not fully recorded.
  • Subsistence output: food grown and eaten at home is never sold, so it is easily missed.
  • Unpaid work: housework and childcare have no market price.
  • Illegal activities: smuggling and other hidden trade are not counted.
  • Weak data: low record-keeping and illiteracy make surveys harder.
  • Double counting: mistakes happen when intermediate goods are not separated from final goods.
  • Price changes: inflation distorts comparisons across years unless real figures are used.

Uses of national income figures include comparing living standards across countries and over time, judging economic growth, guiding planning and showing the shape of the economy by sector.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GDP and GNP?

GDP counts the value of final goods and services produced inside a country's borders, by anyone. GNP counts what is produced by a country's citizens wherever they are, so GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad.

How do you get NNP from GNP?

Subtract depreciation (capital consumption) from GNP. NNP = GNP - depreciation.

What are the three methods of calculating national income?

The output (product) method, the income method and the expenditure method. In theory they give the same total.

What is the difference between nominal and real GDP?

Nominal GDP uses current prices, so it rises with inflation. Real GDP is adjusted for price changes, so it shows true growth in output.

Does a high GDP mean people are well off?

Not necessarily. GDP ignores how income is shared, unpaid work, the informal sector and damage to the environment, so per capita figures and other indicators must be read with it.
