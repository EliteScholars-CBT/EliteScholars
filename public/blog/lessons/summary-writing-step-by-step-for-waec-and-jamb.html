Summary writing step by step: how to pick points and write them in your own words

Summary questions reward students who can find the right points and say them briefly in their own words. Follow this step-by-step method with a fully worked example.

What a summary question really tests

A summary question is not asking you to retell the passage. It tests three things: can you find the points that answer the question, can you shorten them, and can you express them clearly in good English? The marks usually go to the correct points and to the quality of your writing. Always read the exact instruction on your paper, because the number of words or sentences and the part of the passage to use can change from one question to the next.

🔑
Rule one

Answer the question that is asked. If the question says "state the benefits", write benefits only. Do not add causes, problems or examples because they are in the passage.

The method in seven steps

  1. Read the question firstUnderline the key words, such as "benefits", "causes", "problems" or "advice".
  2. Read the passage twiceThe first time for the general idea. The second time with a pencil.
  3. Mark the relevant pointsUnderline only the sentences that match the key words in the question.
  4. Make short notesWrite each point in 3 to 6 words in a list, using your own words.
  5. Delete the extrasRemove examples, repeated ideas, quotations and explanations.
  6. Join the notesWrite them as clear sentences using linking words and correct concord.
  7. Count and checkCount your words, check spelling and punctuation, and compare with the question.

A fully worked example

Read this short passage.

Many Nigerian students fail to read widely because they think reading is only for passing exams. Yet people who read daily improve their vocabulary, write clearer essays and concentrate for longer. A student who reads newspapers learns how facts are reported. A student who reads novels meets new words in context. Some schools have started reading clubs, and the clubs meet twice a week. Members share what they read, and this builds confidence in speaking. Unfortunately, many homes have no books, and electricity is unreliable, so reading at night is difficult. Still, a library card, a phone with offline articles and a small torch can solve most of these problems.

Question: In one sentence of not more than 40 words, state the benefits of reading mentioned in the passage.

QuestionWrite the summary.
  1. Key word in the question: "benefits"
  2. Mark the benefits: improve vocabulary, write clearer essays, concentrate for longer, builds confidence in speaking through reading clubs
  3. Ignore: newspapers and novels (examples), unreliable electricity and the solutions (not benefits)
  4. Notes: wider word knowledge, clearer essays, longer attention, speaking confidence
  5. Join into one sentence and count the words
AnswerRegular reading widens word knowledge, makes essays clearer, lengthens attention span and, through reading clubs, builds confidence in speaking. (21 words)

Notice what happened. The summary has four points, no examples, and it uses its own words: "improve their vocabulary" became "widens word knowledge" and "concentrate for longer" became "lengthens attention span". It is also well within the limit.

Lifting versus own words

Lifted and weak

  • People who read daily improve their vocabulary, write clearer essays and concentrate for longer
  • Some schools have started reading clubs and the clubs meet twice a week

Own words and strong

  • Regular reading widens word knowledge, makes essays clearer and lengthens attention span
  • Reading clubs help members speak with more confidence

You do not have to change every word. Names and technical words can stay. What you must avoid is copying whole stretches of the passage, because the examiner wants to see that you understood it.

Useful techniques

  • Use one verb for several ideas. "It causes hunger, poverty and disease" saves words.
  • Use general words for lists. "Yam, rice and beans" can become "staple foods".
  • Turn clauses into phrases. "Because it rained heavily" becomes "due to heavy rain".
  • Keep the order of the passage unless the question tells you otherwise. It makes your work easier to mark.

Common mistakes are writing an introduction, repeating one point twice in different words, copying the opening line of the passage, and going over the limit. Many marking schemes stop crediting points once you pass the limit, so check the instruction for your exam.

Practice routine

Take a past passage, time yourself for 15 minutes, and follow the seven steps. Then compare your points with the marking scheme or with a teacher. Repeat with a new passage each day, and your selection speed will improve fast. For the exact format and marks, check the official syllabus and the past questions for your exam.

What to do today

  • Pick one past summary question and underline the key words in it
  • Write notes of 3 to 6 words per point before writing sentences
  • Rewrite one lifted sentence in your own words
  • Count the words in your finished answer
  • Ask a friend whether every point answers the question

Frequently asked questions

What is a summary?

A summary is a short, accurate version of a passage that keeps only the main points that answer the question, written mostly in your own words.

Can I copy sentences from the passage?

Avoid copying whole phrases. Lifting is penalised in many marking schemes. Change the wording while keeping the meaning, but keep key technical words that have no simple replacement.

How long should my summary be?

Follow the instruction on the paper exactly. It usually gives a word limit or a number of sentences. Always count your words and stay within the limit.

Should I include examples and illustrations?

Usually no. Summaries need main points, not examples, repetition, quotations or extra explanation.

Do I write the summary in the past or present tense?

Use the tense that suits the passage and be consistent. Write in the third person and in your own sentences, not in the author's voice, unless the question says otherwise.
