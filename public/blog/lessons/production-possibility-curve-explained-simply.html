The Production Possibility Curve Explained Simply (Opportunity Cost, Growth, Efficiency)

The production possibility curve (PPC) shows what an economy can produce when resources are limited. Learn to read it, calculate opportunity cost from it, and explain growth and unemployment on it.

What the production possibility curve shows

Every country has limited land, labour, capital and skills. Because of that, it cannot produce everything it wants. The production possibility curve (also called the production possibility frontier or PPF) is a graph that shows the maximum amounts of two goods an economy can produce at the same time, if all its resources are fully used and the technology stays the same.

Think of a Nigerian farming community with one pool of land and labour. It can grow cocoa or cassava. If it puts everything into cocoa, there is no cassava. If it puts everything into cassava, there is no cocoa. In between, it can mix the two. The curve joins all those best mixes.

🔑
Remember

The PPC is about scarcity and choice. Every point ON the curve is efficient. Every point INSIDE is wasteful. Every point OUTSIDE is not possible with present resources.

A worked example with real numbers

Here is a table for a small economy producing cocoa and cassava (both in thousand tonnes). Each row is a point on the curve.

PointCocoaCassava
A060
B1052
C2040
D3024
E400

Plot cocoa on the horizontal axis and cassava on the vertical axis, mark the five points, and join them with a smooth curve that bends outwards from the origin.

Opportunity cost from the PPC

Opportunity cost is the next best alternative you give up when you make a choice. On a PPC, it is the amount of one good you lose to get more of the other.

QuestionUsing the table, what is the opportunity cost of producing the first 10 thousand tonnes of extra cocoa (A to B), and of the last 10 thousand tonnes (D to E)?
  1. A to B: cocoa rises from 0 to 10 and cassava falls from 60 to 52
  2. Cassava lost = 60 - 52 = 8
  3. D to E: cocoa rises from 30 to 40 and cassava falls from 24 to 0
  4. Cassava lost = 24 - 0 = 24
AnswerThe first 10 tonnes of cocoa cost 8 tonnes of cassava. The last 10 tonnes cost 24 tonnes of cassava.

This is the law of increasing opportunity cost. Land that is perfect for cassava is poor for cocoa, so as we push more resources into cocoa, we have to use land that is better suited to cassava and the sacrifice grows. That is exactly why the curve is bowed outwards (concave to the origin). If resources were equally good at both goods, the PPC would be a straight line and opportunity cost would be constant.

Efficiency, unemployment and impossible points

Look at three types of point:

  • On the curve (such as C: 20 cocoa and 40 cassava): resources are fully and efficiently employed. You cannot gain more of one good without losing some of the other.
  • Inside the curve (such as 15 cocoa and 30 cassava): there is unemployment of resources or inefficiency. You could get more of both goods by using resources better.
  • Outside the curve (such as 30 cocoa and 40 cassava): unattainable for now. It needs more resources or better technology.

Wrong exam answer

  • A point inside the curve means the country is rich
  • Outside the curve means the country is producing too much

Better exam answer

  • Inside means resources are idle or wasted
  • Outside means it is not yet attainable

Typical causes of a point inside the curve are mass unemployment, poor management, strikes, power shortages that keep factories idle, or conflict that stops farming.

Economic growth: shifting the curve

When the economy's capacity to produce rises, the whole PPC shifts outwards. Causes include:

  • Discovery of new resources, such as new farmland or mineral deposits.
  • Better technology, for example improved seeds and machines.
  • More capital goods, such as roads, ports and factories.
  • A larger or better-educated and trained labour force.

If only the technology for cocoa improves, the curve shifts outwards on the cocoa axis only, and the cassava intercept stays where it was. Examiners like this detail. A shift inwards happens when capacity shrinks, for example after floods, war or a wave of skilled workers emigrating.

Common mistakes in PPC questions

  • Saying that a movement along the curve is economic growth. It is not. It is only a different choice. Growth is a shift of the curve.
  • Forgetting that opportunity cost is measured in the other good, not in naira.
  • Drawing the curve touching only one axis, or crossing the axes. It should meet both.
  • Mixing up unemployment (inside the curve) and growth (curve moves).

What to do today

  • Draw your own PPC for two goods you choose
  • Calculate the opportunity cost between three pairs of points
  • Mark one point inside, one on and one outside the curve and explain each
  • Sketch an outward shift and list four causes
  • Write one sentence defining opportunity cost in your own words

Frequently asked questions

What is a production possibility curve?

It is a graph showing the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce when it uses all its resources fully and efficiently with the technology it has.

Why is the production possibility curve bowed outwards?

Because resources are not equally good at making both goods. Moving them from one good to the other gives up more and more of the first good for each extra unit of the second, so opportunity cost rises.

What does a point inside the PPC mean?

It means resources are unemployed or badly used. The economy could produce more of at least one good without giving up any of the other.

What makes the PPC shift outwards?

Economic growth: more resources, better technology, a more skilled labour force or better infrastructure. All of these raise the maximum the economy can produce.
